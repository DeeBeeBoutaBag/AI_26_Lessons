Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 5, Day 1 --- Radical Social Entrepreneurship and Venture Vision
==========================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

Week 5 is the final guided week before Geniuses move into more independent capstone work in Weeks 6 and 7. Day 1 introduces **Radical Social Entrepreneurship** as a way of thinking about building ventures that do more than make money. Geniuses explore how businesses can solve real problems, serve communities with intention, and still build sustainable models that can grow. The lesson emphasizes that strong AI ventures should not just be impressive technically. They should be rooted in real needs, real people, and real impact.

During guided practice, Geniuses work through a **venture vision builder** in Markdown and code-supported project files that helps them define the problem, community, value, impact, and early venture direction for their capstone idea. During independent practice, each Genius creates the first version of a **venture concept brief** for their capstone, which will be expanded throughout Week 5 into a business model, prototype, documentation set, and pitch-ready concept. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel like they are beginning to think not only like builders, but like founders.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Define Radical Social Entrepreneurship and explain how it connects mission, impact, and sustainability.
-   Identify the difference between a cool idea, a useful product, and a venture with real social value.
-   Explain what problem a venture solves, who it serves, and why it matters.
-   Connect an AI product idea to a real community need or challenge.
-   Build a venture vision document in Markdown that defines the foundation of a capstone idea.
-   Reflect on how business, impact, and product design work together in mission-driven ventures.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for reviewing examples if needed
-   Existing product ideas or capstone concepts from earlier weeks
-   Starter folder for venture documents
-   Markdown preview in VS Code

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Define a real-world problem and connect it to a structured solution.
-   Computer Science Practices: Design technology projects that respond to community needs and intended outcomes.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate how technology can create impact, opportunity, and responsibility.
-   Career Readiness: Practice mission setting, strategic thinking, communication, and venture framing.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Develop a venture idea that connects user need, social impact, and sustainability.
-   Product Development: Articulate a product's purpose, users, and value clearly.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that entrepreneurship can be about solving meaningful problems, not just building hype.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Big Idea, Real Problem, or Just Hype?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label three parts of the room:
    -   **Real Problem**
    -   **Interesting Idea**
    -   **Mostly Hype**
-   Read venture ideas aloud and have Geniuses move to where they think the idea belongs.
-   Example prompts:
    -   An AI app that helps students organize assignments across multiple classes
    -   A chatbot that gives random compliments
    -   An AI tool that helps families translate confusing school or medical language into simpler explanations
    -   A social media app where AI writes captions in your "perfect vibe"
    -   An AI system that helps small community organizations manage volunteers and events
    -   A tool that generates fake engagement to make people look more popular online
    -   An AI career guide for first-generation students navigating internships
    -   An AI website that makes memes from anything you type
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What makes this a real problem?
    -   Who is actually helped here?
    -   What makes this feel useful or not useful?
    -   Could this become a mission-driven business?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Venture Sorting**

-   Put venture ideas on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to sort them into:
    -   strong social impact
    -   useful but not mission-driven
    -   weak or mostly hype
-   Discuss where the differences show up.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A strong founder does not just chase what is flashy. They notice what matters, who is underserved, and what kind of venture could make a real difference."
-   Ask:
    -   What makes a problem worth building around?
    -   What makes a venture more than just a product?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand Radical Social Entrepreneurship and how a mission-driven venture is shaped.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and pause often for examples and discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Week 5 Focus --- From Product Builder to Venture Builder**
    -   This week is about turning capstone ideas into stronger ventures
    -   Geniuses are preparing for Weeks 6 and 7
-   **Slide 2: What Is Radical Social Entrepreneurship?**
    -   Building ventures that solve real problems while centering people, justice, access, and sustainability
    -   Not just profit
    -   Not just good intentions
    -   Mission plus execution
-   **Slide 3: What Makes a Venture Socially Grounded?**
    -   It addresses a real problem
    -   It serves a real group of people
    -   It creates meaningful value
    -   It thinks about impact, not just attention
    -   It aims to sustain itself over time
-   **Slide 4: Mission, Product, and Business**
    -   **Mission:** Why does this matter?
    -   **Product:** What are we building?
    -   **Business:** How does this survive and grow?
-   **Slide 5: A Cool Tool Is Not Always a Venture**
    -   A venture needs:
        -   a clear problem
        -   a defined user
        -   a reason people care
        -   a path to value
        -   a reason it should exist
-   **Slide 6: Signs of a Strong Venture Direction**
    -   urgent problem
    -   clear audience
    -   social value
    -   realistic use case
    -   potential for sustainability
-   **Slide 7: Questions Founders Should Ask**
    -   Who is this for?
    -   What pain point is real here?
    -   Why now?
    -   What changes if this works?
    -   Why is AI actually useful here?
    -   How could this create both impact and sustainability?
-   **Slide 8: Social Impact and Business Are Not Opposites**
    -   Mission-driven ventures still need:
        -   strong value proposition
        -   healthy business model
        -   clear operations
        -   adoption and trust
-   **Slide 9: Capstone Thinking Starts Here**
    -   This week builds the venture layer:
        -   mission
        -   business design
        -   prototype communication
        -   documentation
        -   pitch readiness
-   **Slide 10: Day 1 Goal**
    -   Define the venture vision clearly enough that someone else can understand the problem, the user, and the mission

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes a venture socially meaningful?
-   Can a business make money and still be mission-driven?
-   Why is solving a real problem more important than sounding innovative?
-   What kinds of communities are often underserved by technology?
-   How can AI be useful without becoming a gimmick?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "What is one real problem in school, family life, work, or community life that deserves a serious solution, and why?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 55 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses create a first draft of a venture vision document for a mission-driven AI business idea.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is about founder thinking, not just feature thinking.
-   Geniuses will create a simple venture brief in Markdown.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Create a folder called `venture-vision-builder`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a Markdown document called `venture_vision.md` that defines:

-   venture name
-   problem
-   who it serves
-   why the problem matters
-   why AI is useful
-   what social impact it could create
-   what success would look like

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open VS Code.
2.  Create a folder called `venture-vision-builder`.
3.  Inside the folder, create:
    -   `venture_vision.md`
    -   optional `notes.js` if Geniuses want a small helper script for idea prompts
4.  Open Markdown preview in VS Code.

#### **Sample `venture_vision.md`**
```
# Venture Vision: BridgePath AI

## Problem
Many first-generation high school students do not have easy access to clear, personalized guidance about internships, career pathways, and professional opportunities.

## Who It Serves
BridgePath AI serves high school Geniuses, especially first-generation students who may not have built-in access to career networks, mentors, or professional guidance.

## Why This Problem Matters
Without guidance, many students miss opportunities, feel unsure of their next steps, and may not see career pathways that match their strengths and interests.

## Product Idea
BridgePath AI is an AI-powered career support platform that helps Geniuses explore opportunities, prepare for internships, practice professional communication, and organize next steps.

## Why AI Is Useful Here
AI can provide personalized guidance, explain opportunities in clear language, generate practice resources, and help students move faster with support that is available when they need it.

## Social Impact
This venture could help make career access more equitable by giving more Geniuses tools, clarity, and confidence as they prepare for the future.

## What Success Looks Like
- More Geniuses applying for internships and opportunities
- More confidence in career exploration
- Better access to practical career guidance
- A platform that schools, programs, or youth organizations could actually use
```
#### **Optional Helper Script: `notes.js`**
```
// notes.js

// This script prints venture vision prompts to help Geniuses think like founders.

const prompts = [
  "What real problem does your venture solve?",
  "Who feels this problem most strongly?",
  "Why does this problem matter right now?",
  "What would change if your venture worked well?",
  "Why is AI actually useful in this solution?",
  "What social good could come from this venture?"
];

console.log("Venture Vision Prompts:\n");

prompts.forEach((prompt, index) => {
  console.log(`${index + 1}. ${prompt}`);
});
```
#### **How to Run the Helper Script**
```
node notes.js
```
#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why a venture should start with the problem, not just the feature
-   Why mission and user need must be explicit
-   Why AI should only be used when it adds real value
-   Why founders need to explain both product value and social impact
-   Why clarity in writing strengthens clarity in building

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   Which part of the venture vision was hardest to define?
-   What made the example venture feel real?
-   What makes a problem statement strong?
-   Why should founders be specific about who they serve?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently draft the first version of their capstone venture vision.
-   This begins the Week 5 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**Capstone Venture Package --- Day 1 Venture Vision**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius creates their own `venture_vision.md` and defines the foundation of their venture.

Their document must include:

-   venture name
-   problem statement
-   target user or community
-   why the problem matters
-   product idea
-   why AI is useful
-   social impact
-   what success looks like

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A completed `venture_vision.md`
-   Optional `notes.js` or another simple helper file if they want prompt support
-   A venture concept with:
    -   a clear problem
    -   a defined user or community
    -   a mission-driven angle
    -   a reason AI is relevant
    -   a picture of impact and success
-   A short written reflection:
    -   Why is this venture worth building?
    -   What makes it more than just a cool tech idea?
    -   What part of the venture vision still needs more thought?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses choose a real problem and a clear audience
-   Ask:
    -   Who really needs this?
    -   Why does this matter in real life?
    -   What makes this socially meaningful?
    -   Would this still matter if AI were not trendy?
-   Push Geniuses toward specificity and away from vague startup language

### 5\. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that great ventures begin with a strong mission, a real problem, and a clear reason to exist.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to share:
    -   their venture name
    -   the problem they are solving
    -   who they want to serve
    -   one reason the venture matters
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a venture feel mission-driven?
    -   What is the difference between a product and a venture?
    -   Why is clarity on the problem so important before building further?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will develop business models, value propositions, and business canvases to make the venture more complete.

**Assessment:**
---------------

Student learning will be assessed through:

-   Participation in the icebreaker and discussion
-   Reflection responses during direct instruction
-   Completion of the guided venture vision document
-   Completion of an independent venture vision for the capstone
-   Final share-out and explanation of problem, audience, and mission

### **Exit Ticket**

1.  What is Radical Social Entrepreneurship?

---------------------

    -   add an early tagline or one-sentence pitch

    -   begin sketching what success metrics could look like

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses realize that founding a venture is not just about having a tool or an app. It is about having a reason to exist. Day 1 of Week 5 sets the tone for the capstone transition by helping Geniuses connect mission, people, product, and impact. By the end of the lesson, they should feel that they are not only building something functional. They are beginning to define something meaningful, sustainable, and worth developing further.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that mission-driven ventures still need clear execution.
-   Encourage specificity over vague inspirational language.
-   Help Geniuses connect AI to real usefulness, not just novelty.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will turn this venture vision into a clearer business model and canvas.

-   A clear explanation of why the problem matters
