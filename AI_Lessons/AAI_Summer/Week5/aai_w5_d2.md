Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 5, Day 2 --- Business Models, Value Propositions, and Business Canvases
==================================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

On Day 2, Geniuses move from venture vision into business design. After identifying a real problem, a real community, and a mission-driven direction on Day 1, Geniuses now focus on how their venture creates value, delivers value, and sustains itself over time. This lesson introduces core startup thinking such as **value propositions, customer segments, channels, revenue streams, cost structure, and business model canvases**. Geniuses learn that strong ventures are not only powered by good intentions or strong products. They also need a model for how the solution reaches people and remains sustainable.

During guided practice, the class builds a capstone-ready **business model canvas** in Markdown and structured planning files. Geniuses break down who their users are, what value they offer, how they reach those users, and how the venture could operate as a real business. During independent practice, each Genius completes the first version of a business model canvas for their capstone venture and refines their value proposition so it is clearer, more specific, and more realistic. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should be thinking like venture designers, not only mission-driven founders.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Explain what a business model is and why it matters for mission-driven ventures.
-   Define a value proposition and explain how it connects a product to a user need.
-   Identify the key parts of a business model canvas.
-   Distinguish between customer segments, users, channels, revenue streams, and cost structure.
-   Build a business model canvas in Markdown for an AI venture.
-   Refine a venture's value proposition so it is clear, specific, and connected to real users.
-   Reflect on how sustainability and impact work together in socially grounded businesses.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Existing `venture_vision.md` from Day 1
-   Starter folder for business planning files
-   Markdown preview in VS Code

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Break down a venture into interconnected components such as users, value, delivery, and sustainability.
-   Computer Science Practices: Design technical ventures with clear structure, purpose, and logic beyond the code.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate how digital products are delivered, adopted, and sustained.
-   Career Readiness: Practice strategic thinking, business communication, and systems planning.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Design a viable business model for a mission-driven AI venture.
-   Product Development: Define how a product creates value for specific users and stakeholders.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that even powerful, meaningful products need a real model for how they work as ventures.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Who Pays, Who Uses, Who Benefits?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label three parts of the room:
    -   **User**
    -   **Customer**
    -   **Beneficiary**
-   Read venture scenarios aloud and have Geniuses move to the part of the room that answers the question being asked.
-   Example prompts:
    -   "A school district pays for an AI literacy platform used by students"
    -   "A nonprofit licenses an AI volunteer management tool used by staff"
    -   "Parents subscribe to an AI study planner used by their child"
    -   "A community center uses a free AI translation tool built for families"
-   After each prompt, ask:
    -   Who actually uses it?
    -   Who might pay for it?
    -   Who benefits even if they are not the customer?
    -   Why is this difference important?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Business Role Sort**

-   Put venture scenarios on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to identify:
    -   the user
    -   the customer
    -   the main beneficiary
-   Discuss how some ventures have different people in each role.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A venture is not only about the product. It is also about how value moves."
-   Ask:
    -   Who actually uses your product?
    -   Who might support it, pay for it, or help it spread?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand how business models work and how business canvases help founders think clearly.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and pause often for discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Day 2 Focus --- Designing the Business Behind the Venture**
    -   Today is about how the venture creates, delivers, and sustains value
-   **Slide 2: What Is a Business Model?**
    -   A business model explains:
        -   who the venture serves
        -   what value it provides
        -   how it reaches people
        -   how it sustains itself
-   **Slide 3: What Is a Value Proposition?**
    -   A value proposition explains:
        -   who the product is for
        -   what problem it solves
        -   why it is useful or better
    -   Strong value propositions are specific and clear
-   **Slide 4: Common Business Model Canvas Blocks**
    -   Customer Segments
    -   Value Proposition
    -   Channels
    -   Customer Relationships
    -   Revenue Streams
    -   Key Resources
    -   Key Activities
    -   Key Partners
    -   Cost Structure
-   **Slide 5: Customer Segment vs User**
    -   Sometimes the user and the customer are the same
    -   Sometimes they are different
    -   This matters for design and pricing
-   **Slide 6: Channels**
    -   How do people discover and access the product?
    -   Examples:
        -   direct signup
        -   schools
        -   community organizations
        -   partnerships
        -   social media
        -   referrals
-   **Slide 7: Revenue Streams**
    -   subscriptions
    -   licensing
    -   freemium upgrades
    -   institutional partnerships
    -   service contracts
    -   sponsored access
    -   paid premium tools
-   **Slide 8: Cost Structure**
    -   API costs
    -   hosting
    -   design and development
    -   support
    -   marketing
    -   outreach
    -   content or training
-   **Slide 9: Mission and Sustainability Must Work Together**
    -   A socially grounded venture still needs a way to survive
    -   Sustainability helps the impact continue
-   **Slide 10: Day 2 Goal**
    -   Turn your venture vision into a clearer business model and first canvas

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   Why do founders need to think about business models early?
-   What makes a value proposition strong?
-   Why are users and customers sometimes different?
-   What kinds of revenue models fit mission-driven ventures?
-   How can a venture stay community-centered without ignoring sustainability?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "Who is the main user of your venture, who might pay for it, and what makes your solution valuable enough to support?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 55 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses build the first version of a business model canvas for a mission-driven AI venture.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is about structuring the business side of the idea clearly.
-   Geniuses will create a Markdown file called `business_model_canvas.md`.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Create a folder called `venture-business-model`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a Markdown document that outlines the key parts of the venture's business model.

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open VS Code.
2.  Create a folder called `venture-business-model`.
3.  Inside the folder, create:
    -   `business_model_canvas.md`
    -   optional `canvas_prompts.js`
4.  Open Markdown preview in VS Code.

#### **Sample `business_model_canvas.md`**
```
# Business Model Canvas: BridgePath AI

## Customer Segments
- High school Geniuses exploring internships and career pathways
- Schools and youth programs that want stronger career readiness support

## Value Proposition
BridgePath AI helps Geniuses explore career pathways, prepare for opportunities, and organize next steps with accessible, personalized support.

## Channels
- School partnerships
- Youth development programs
- Program staff referrals
- Social media outreach
- Workshop demonstrations

## Customer Relationships
- Guided onboarding
- Helpful AI support
- Trust-building notes and clear explanations
- Educator or program-facing support materials

## Revenue Streams
- School or program subscriptions
- Licensing for youth organizations
- Freemium student access with premium features later
- Sponsored access through partners

## Key Resources
- AI API access
- Product design and development
- Career guidance content
- School and program relationships
- User trust and adoption

## Key Activities
- Product development
- AI workflow improvement
- User testing
- Outreach to schools and programs
- Support and iteration

## Key Partners
- Schools
- Youth organizations
- Mentors and career programs
- Community-based partners
- Internship and workforce readiness networks

## Cost Structure
- API usage
- Hosting
- Product development time
- Outreach and partnership development
- Ongoing support and iteration
```
#### **Optional Helper Script: `canvas_prompts.js`**
```
// canvas_prompts.js
// Applied AI Week 5 Day 2
// This script prints prompts to help Geniuses fill out a business model canvas.

const prompts = [
  "Who are the main users of your product?",
  "Who might actually pay for it?",
  "What problem are you solving better than current options?",
  "How will people discover or access your venture?",
  "What are possible ways the venture could sustain itself financially?",
  "What key resources are needed to make this venture work?",
  "Who could be strong partners for this venture?",
  "What ongoing costs might the venture have?"
];

console.log("Business Model Canvas Prompts:\n");

prompts.forEach((prompt, index) => {
  console.log(`${index + 1}. ${prompt}`);
});
```
#### **How to Run the Helper Script**
```
node canvas_prompts.js
```
#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why the value proposition needs to be user-centered
-   Why channels and revenue are not the same thing
-   Why key partners matter for mission-driven ventures
-   Why sustainable models strengthen long-term impact
-   Why the business canvas helps founders see the whole venture, not just the product

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   Which canvas section felt easiest to define?
-   Which section felt hardest?
-   Why is revenue thinking uncomfortable for some mission-driven builders?
-   What makes a business model realistic rather than only inspirational?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently create the first business model canvas for their capstone venture.
-   This continues the Week 5 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**Capstone Venture Package --- Day 2 Business Model Canvas**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius creates their own `business_model_canvas.md` and completes the first business design for their venture.

Their document must include:

-   customer segments
-   value proposition
-   channels
-   customer relationships
-   revenue streams
-   key resources
-   key activities
-   key partners
-   cost structure

They must also refine their value proposition into a strong one- to two-sentence statement.

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A completed `business_model_canvas.md`
-   Optional `canvas_prompts.js` or another helper script if useful
-   A refined value proposition
-   A business model that includes:
    -   the user
    -   the customer
    -   the delivery path
    -   the sustainability path
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What part of your business model feels strongest?
    -   What part still feels uncertain?
    -   How does the model support both mission and sustainability?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses separate users from customers when needed
-   Ask:
    -   Who gets the most direct value?
    -   Who has the power to pay, adopt, or distribute this?
    -   What channel feels most realistic?
    -   What costs will exist even before profit?
-   Encourage realistic thinking without shrinking the mission

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that strong ventures need a clear model for how value moves and how the business survives.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to share:
    -   their venture name
    -   their value proposition
    -   one likely customer segment
    -   one possible revenue model
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a business model strong?
    -   Why should mission-driven ventures still think seriously about revenue?
    -   What part of the canvas helped you understand your venture better?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will prototype the venture experience more clearly so someone else can see, understand, and react to the idea.

**Assessment:**
---------------

Student learning will be assessed through:

-   Participation in the icebreaker and discussion
-   Reflection responses during direct instruction
-   Completion of the guided business model canvas
-   Completion of an independent business model canvas for the capstone
-   Final share-out and explanation of value proposition and business design

### **Exit Ticket**

1.  What is a business model?
2.  What is a value proposition?
3.  What is one realistic way your venture could sustain itself?

**Extra Activities:**
---------------------

-   Geniuses who finish early can:
    -   add a "Why Us?" section to the canvas
    -   add multiple customer segments and rank them
    -   add multiple possible revenue models and compare them
    -   add a short "early adoption strategy" section
    -   add a "biggest business risk" section
    -   create a one-sentence pitch based on the canvas
-   Add a "Why now?" section
-   Add an "Unfair advantage" section
-   Add a "First partnership target" section
-   Add a "What would we test first?" section

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses understand that great ventures do not only have a strong mission. They also have a clear model for how value is created, delivered, and sustained. Day 2 helps Geniuses see the business side of their capstone idea as something thoughtful and structured, not separate from their mission. By the end of the lesson, they should feel that their venture has moved from an inspiring idea toward something more real, more testable, and more sustainable.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that sustainability strengthens impact rather than weakening it.
-   Encourage specificity over broad startup language.
-   Help Geniuses stay realistic while still thinking boldly.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will move into prototyping the venture experience more clearly.

----------------------------------------

-   A completed business model canvas
-   A clearer value proposition
-   Defined user and customer segments
-   Possible channels and revenue streams
-   A first look at key resources, activities, partners, and costs
-   A stronger foundation for prototyping and pitch development later in the week
