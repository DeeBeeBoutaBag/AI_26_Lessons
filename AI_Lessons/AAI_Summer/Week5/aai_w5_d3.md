Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 5, Day 3 --- Prototyping the Venture Experience
==========================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

On Day 3, Geniuses move from venture vision and business modeling into **prototyping**. The focus of the lesson is helping Geniuses understand that a prototype is not just a design artifact. It is a communication tool that shows how a venture works, what users experience, and why the solution matters. Geniuses learn that strong founders do not only describe their ventures well. They make them visible, testable, and easier for others to understand.

This lesson centers on translating an AI venture into a clearer product experience. Geniuses use HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and, when useful, OpenAI-powered functionality to improve or create a venture prototype that communicates the user journey, product value, and mission clearly. During guided practice, the class builds a stronger venture prototype page that includes a hero section, value proposition, user flow, and interactive demo area. During independent practice, each Genius improves or creates a capstone-ready prototype for their venture. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should have a prototype that makes the venture easier to understand, test, and present.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Explain what a prototype is and why it matters in venture building.
-   Distinguish between a product idea, a business model, and a prototype experience.
-   Identify the core elements a prototype should communicate, including problem, user, value, and interaction.
-   Build or improve a venture prototype using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Create a prototype that communicates the user journey and value proposition clearly.
-   Reflect on how prototyping helps founders test ideas, gather feedback, and strengthen pitches.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for testing local websites
-   Existing venture documents from Days 1 and 2
-   Existing HTML/CSS/JS product files from earlier weeks, if available
-   Starter folder for prototype files

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Translate a venture concept into a structured user experience.
-   Computer Science Practices: Build and refine an interactive prototype that communicates product value.
-   Web Development: Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to create a venture prototype.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate how digital prototypes communicate usefulness, trust, and user flow.
-   Career Readiness: Practice prototyping, iteration, communication, and founder storytelling.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Build a venture prototype that helps others understand, test, and believe in the idea.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that prototypes help people experience a venture, not just hear about it.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Pitch It or Show It?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Pitch It"** and the other side **"Show It."**
-   Read venture scenarios aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches what would help someone understand the idea better.
-   Example prompts:
    -   A founder explains an AI study helper in one paragraph
    -   A founder shows a page where a Genius can type assignments and see a plan appear
    -   A founder describes an AI career app with a strong mission statement
    -   A founder shows the user journey from entering interests to getting career suggestions
    -   A founder says their product is "like Uber for AI support"
    -   A founder shows a landing page, user flow, and result screen
-   After each round, ask:
    -   Why would showing this help more than only explaining it?
    -   What should a prototype prove?
    -   What would make someone believe the product is real?
    -   What still needs to be spoken even if the prototype looks good?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Prototype Critique**

-   Show two early-stage venture examples:
    -   one that is mostly words
    -   one that includes a simple but clear prototype
-   Ask Geniuses:
    -   Which one is easier to understand?
    -   Which one feels more believable?
    -   Which one would make it easier to get feedback?

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A prototype turns the venture from an idea into an experience."
-   Ask:
    -   What should someone understand after seeing your prototype?
    -   What part of your venture needs to be shown, not just described?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand what prototypes do, what they should include, and how they support venture growth.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and pause often for discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Day 3 Focus --- Make the Venture Visible**
    -   Today is about turning the venture into something people can see, use, and react to
-   **Slide 2: What Is a Prototype?**
    -   A prototype is a draft version of the product experience
    -   It helps people understand:
        -   what the product is
        -   what it does
        -   who it is for
        -   how it feels to use
-   **Slide 3: Why Prototypes Matter**
    -   they make ideas clearer
    -   they help gather feedback
    -   they make pitches stronger
    -   they reveal what still feels confusing
    -   they help founders test assumptions
-   **Slide 4: What a Good Venture Prototype Should Communicate**
    -   the problem
    -   the user
    -   the value
    -   the experience
    -   the outcome
    -   the reason the venture matters
-   **Slide 5: Prototype Levels**
    -   low fidelity:
        -   sketches
        -   layouts
        -   rough flows
    -   medium fidelity:
        -   cleaner screens
        -   stronger visual hierarchy
        -   some interaction
    -   coded prototype:
        -   working buttons
        -   flows
        -   demo interactions
        -   possibly AI-powered behavior
-   **Slide 6: What Makes a Prototype Useful?**
    -   clear purpose
    -   clear user path
    -   focused feature set
    -   understandable value
    -   realistic enough to react to
-   **Slide 7: Common Prototype Sections**
    -   landing or hero section
    -   who it is for
    -   problem and value
    -   feature or workflow section
    -   demo area
    -   trust or mission note
    -   call to action
-   **Slide 8: Prototype Mistakes**
    -   trying to show everything
    -   unclear headline
    -   no real user flow
    -   weak explanation of the value
    -   good visuals but no substance
    -   too much text and not enough interaction
-   **Slide 9: Day 3 Goal**
    -   build or improve a capstone prototype that someone else can understand quickly
-   **Slide 10: Looking Ahead**
    -   tomorrow Geniuses will write README files, tech docs, and stronger venture documentation

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What should a prototype prove?
-   Why is showing a user flow more powerful than describing one?
-   What makes a prototype feel believable?
-   What should be included, and what can be left out?
-   Why is it better to make one part clear than try to prototype everything at once?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "What part of your venture most needs to be shown in a prototype so people understand why it matters?"

#### **Sample `script.js`**
```
// script.js
// Applied AI Week 5 Day 3
// This script adds simple interactivity to the venture prototype.

const userInput = document.getElementById("userInput");
const generateButton = document.getElementById("generateButton");
const clearButton = document.getElementById("clearButton");
const statusMessage = document.getElementById("statusMessage");
const resultSection = document.getElementById("resultSection");
const resultOutput = document.getElementById("resultOutput");

generateButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const text = userInput.value.trim();

  if (text === "") {
    statusMessage.textContent = "Please enter your interests first.";
    resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
    return;
  }

  statusMessage.textContent = "Generating prototype response...";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");

  setTimeout(() => {
    resultOutput.textContent =
      "Based on your interests, possible directions could include software engineering, user experience design, product management, nonprofit technology, or community-centered innovation roles.";
    statusMessage.textContent = "Prototype response ready.";
    resultSection.classList.remove("hidden");
  }, 700);
});

clearButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  userInput.value = "";
  resultOutput.textContent = "";
  statusMessage.textContent = "";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
});
```

-   What made this prototype easy to understand?
-   What part of the venture became clearer once it was shown visually?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently create or improve a capstone-ready prototype that communicates their venture clearly.
-   This continues the Week 5 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**Capstone Venture Package --- Day 3 Venture Prototype**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius creates or improves a venture prototype for their capstone.

Their prototype should communicate:

-   venture name
-   who it is for
-   the problem
-   the value
-   a simple user flow
-   at least one interactive or visual prototype element

Their prototype can be:

-   a landing page
-   a product demo page
-   a workflow page
-   a simplified web experience showing the main product path

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working `index.html`
-   A working `style.css`
-   A working `script.js`
-   A venture prototype that includes:
    -   clear headline
    -   value proposition
    -   user or community focus
    -   problem statement
    -   one prototype interaction or simulated product behavior
    -   one mission or trust-oriented section
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What does your prototype help people understand?
    -   What part of your venture became clearer through prototyping?
    -   What still needs improvement?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses focus on showing the most important part of the venture
-   Ask:
    -   What should someone understand in the first 15 seconds?
    -   What feature or flow matters most to show?
    -   Does the prototype feel connected to the user and mission?
    -   What could be simplified?
-   Encourage clarity, hierarchy, and strong storytelling

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that prototypes make ventures more understandable, testable, and believable.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to show:
    -   their venture name
    -   one part of the prototype they are most proud of
    -   one thing the prototype helps people understand better
    -   one thing they still want to improve
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a prototype effective?
    -   Why is it important to show the user journey?
    -   How does prototyping help a founder think more clearly?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will create README files, technical docs, and product documentation so their ventures can be explained clearly to others.

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses understand that a strong venture needs to be seen, not only described. Prototypes help others understand the product, react to the user journey, and believe the venture has potential. Day 3 helps Geniuses move their capstone ideas closer to something real and presentable. By the end of the lesson, they should feel that their venture is no longer only a concept document. It is becoming an experience someone else can interact with, react to, and support.

-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will write README files and technical documentation to support their capstone.
