Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 3, Day 3 --- User Insight, Feedback, and Designing AI Products With Real User Voices
===============================================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

On Day 3, Geniuses focus on one of the most important parts of strong product design: learning from users. After exploring UI/UX and culturally rooted design, Geniuses now examine how real user insight should shape AI products. This lesson introduces user feedback, observation, interviews, pain points, and product revision through a user-centered lens. Geniuses learn that strong builders do not guess what users need forever. They ask, test, listen, and improve.

During guided practice, the class builds a simple user feedback collector and insight display using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Geniuses also practice writing better user interview questions and identifying the difference between surface feedback and real insight. During independent practice, each Genius gathers, organizes, or simulates user feedback for their Week 3 mini-project and uses that insight to improve their product interface, wording, flow, or trust features. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should see that better AI products come from better listening.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Explain why user insight matters in the design of AI products.
-   Distinguish between opinions, complaints, patterns, and useful product insight.
-   Write stronger user interview and feedback questions.
-   Identify user pain points, confusion points, and unmet needs.
-   Build a simple feedback collection and display interface using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Improve an AI product based on real or simulated user feedback.
-   Reflect on how listening to users improves clarity, trust, and product usefulness.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for viewing local web apps
-   Existing Week 3 project files
-   Optional printed interview question stems
-   Optional peer feedback form or sticky notes for critique

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Analyze user behavior, feedback, and friction points to improve digital systems.
-   Computer Science Practices: Test and improve a product through observation, critique, and iteration.
-   Web Development: Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to support product feedback and insight gathering.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate product design through a user-centered and trust-centered lens.
-   Career Readiness: Practice listening, empathy, questioning, critique, and iterative improvement.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Improve a product by understanding real users and acting on meaningful insight.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that strong products are shaped by what users actually experience, not only what builders assume.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Good Feedback or Weak Feedback?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Useful Insight"** and the other side **"Weak Feedback."**
-   Read feedback statements aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches.
-   Example prompts:
    -   "This is cool."
    -   "I do not know what button to press first."
    -   "The colors are weird."
    -   "I was confused about what kind of answer I was supposed to type."
    -   "I like it."
    -   "The output felt helpful, but I did not trust whether it was accurate."
    -   "I did not understand what the product was for until I scrolled down."
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What makes this useful or weak?
    -   What can a builder actually do with this feedback?
    -   What question would help you learn more?
    -   What pattern might this reveal?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Rapid Critique Sorting**

-   Put several feedback statements on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to sort them into:
    -   vague opinion
    -   specific product issue
    -   deeper user insight
-   Discuss why specific feedback helps builders improve faster.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "The goal is not just to hear feedback. The goal is to find insight that tells you how the product should improve."
-   Ask:
    -   What is the difference between a comment and an insight?
    -   Why do builders need user voices instead of only their own ideas?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand what user insight is, how to gather it, and how to use it to improve AI products.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and pause often for examples and discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Day 3 Focus --- Build With Users, Not Just For Them**
    -   Strong products come from listening, testing, and improving
-   **Slide 2: What Is User Insight?**
    -   User insight is more than feedback
    -   It is a deeper understanding of:
        -   what users need
        -   where they struggle
        -   what they expect
        -   what they feel
        -   what they do not trust
        -   what they wish was easier
-   **Slide 3: Feedback vs Insight**
    -   Feedback: "This is confusing"
    -   Insight: "Users do not understand the first step because the page does not explain what to enter"
    -   Insight gives direction
-   **Slide 4: Why User Insight Matters in AI Products**
    -   AI products can feel uncertain
    -   Users may not know what to expect
    -   Builders need to understand:
        -   where users hesitate
        -   where users mistrust the output
        -   where users get lost
        -   what users really want help with
-   **Slide 5: Common Ways to Gather User Insight**
    -   interviews
    -   observation
    -   peer testing
    -   surveys
    -   think-aloud sessions
    -   product walkthroughs
-   **Slide 6: Strong User Interview Questions**
    -   What were you trying to do?
    -   What part felt confusing?
    -   What did you expect to happen?
    -   What made you trust or distrust the result?
    -   What would make this more useful for you?
    -   What felt missing?
-   **Slide 7: Weak User Interview Questions**
    -   Did you like it?
    -   Was it good?
    -   Would you use it?
    -   These often lead to shallow answers
-   **Slide 8: Patterns Matter More Than One Comment**
    -   one opinion is not always the truth
    -   repeated friction points matter
    -   look for themes:
        -   confusion
        -   trust
        -   clarity
        -   usefulness
        -   relevance
-   **Slide 9: Turning Insight Into Design Changes**
    -   confusing input → improve instructions
    -   weak trust → add examples and transparency
    -   weak flow → simplify steps
    -   generic language → redesign for the real audience
-   **Slide 10: Week 3 Mini-Project Growth**
    -   Day 1: UX clarity
    -   Day 2: cultural grounding
    -   Day 3: user insight and product improvement

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   Why do builders sometimes miss what users really need?
-   What makes an interview question strong?
-   Why is "I like it" usually not enough?
-   What kinds of user insight matter most for AI products?
-   How can feedback improve trust, not just design?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "If someone tested your product today, what do you think they might get confused by or want improved?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 55 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses build a simple feedback collection interface and practice turning comments into product insight.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is about learning how to listen, organize, and act on what users say.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Use the existing Week 3 project or create a folder called `user-insight-lab`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a simple product feedback page that:

-   lets a user enter their name
-   lets a user write feedback
-   lets a user choose a feedback category
-   displays feedback on the page
-   helps organize responses into useful insight themes

#### **Sample `script.js`**
```
// script.js
// Applied AI Week 3 Day 3
// This script collects and displays user feedback for product insight practice.

const userName = document.getElementById("userName");
const feedbackType = document.getElementById("feedbackType");
const feedbackText = document.getElementById("feedbackText");
const saveFeedbackButton = document.getElementById("saveFeedbackButton");
const clearFeedbackButton = document.getElementById("clearFeedbackButton");
const statusMessage = document.getElementById("statusMessage");
const feedbackList = document.getElementById("feedbackList");

// Store feedback items in an array
const feedbackItems = [];

saveFeedbackButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const name = userName.value.trim();
  const type = feedbackType.value;
  const text = feedbackText.value.trim();

  if (name === "" || text === "") {
    statusMessage.textContent = "Please complete the name and feedback fields first.";
    return;
  }

  const feedbackItem = {
    name: name,
    type: type,
    text: text,
  };

  feedbackItems.push(feedbackItem);
  renderFeedback();

  statusMessage.textContent = "Feedback saved.";

  userName.value = "";
  feedbackType.value = "clarity";
  feedbackText.value = "";
});

clearFeedbackButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  userName.value = "";
  feedbackType.value = "clarity";
  feedbackText.value = "";
  statusMessage.textContent = "";
});

function renderFeedback() {
  feedbackList.innerHTML = "";

  feedbackItems.forEach((item) => {
    const card = document.createElement("div");
    card.classList.add("feedback-card");

    card.innerHTML = `
 <h3>${item.name}</h3>
 <span class="feedback-tag">${item.type}</span>
 <p>${item.text}</p>
 `;

    feedbackList.appendChild(card);
  });
}
```

-   Why collecting feedback is different from finding insight
-   Why categories help builders notice patterns
-   Why strong feedback questions lead to stronger product improvements
-   Why repeated user friction matters
-   Why product teams need a system for organizing what they learn

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   What kind of feedback here is most useful?
-   Which category might reveal the biggest product problem?
-   What repeated pattern would worry you most as a builder?
-   How could feedback directly change the product?

### 4\. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently gather, organize, or simulate user feedback and use it to improve their product.
-   This continues the Week 3 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**AI Product Redesign --- Day 3 User Insight and Feedback**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius uses one of these paths:

-   **Option A: Peer Feedback**
    -   Have another Genius test the product
    -   Ask 3--5 strong interview questions
    -   Record their answers
    -   Identify at least 2 useful insights
-   **Option B: Simulated User Insight**
    -   Create 3 realistic user feedback entries based on the intended audience
    -   Sort them into categories
    -   Identify at least 2 patterns
    -   Improve the product based on those patterns

Then each Genius must make at least **2 product changes** based on insight.

Examples:

-   better instructions
-   clearer button text
-   stronger trust note
-   better example input
-   more specific onboarding language
-   simpler result layout

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   Updated `index.html`
-   Updated `style.css`
-   Updated `script.js`
-   A feedback or insight log that includes:
    -   at least 3 feedback entries
    -   categories or themes
    -   at least 2 insights
-   At least 2 product changes based on what was learned
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What insight did you learn?
    -   What did you change because of it?
    -   How does the product better serve the user now?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Push Geniuses to go beyond vague observations
-   Ask:
    -   What exactly was the user confused by?
    -   What repeated pattern are you noticing?
    -   What change would directly address that?
    -   What does this tell you about your user's needs?
-   Encourage specific, actionable revisions

### 5\. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that strong AI products are shaped by real user voices and thoughtful revision.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to share:
    -   one piece of feedback they got
    -   one insight they discovered
    -   one product change they made
    -   one thing they still want to learn from users
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What is the difference between feedback and insight?
    -   Why do strong builders need to listen more than they assume?
    -   How can user insight improve trust in AI products?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will redesign product flows and wireframes more intentionally based on everything they have learned about UX, culture, and users.

Student learning will be assessed through:

-   Participation in the icebreaker and discussion
-   Reflection responses during direct instruction
-   Completion of the guided feedback collection build
-   Completion of an insight log or peer feedback process
-   Completion of at least 2 product changes based on insight
-   Final share-out and explanation of product improvements

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses understand that feedback is not just something you collect to say you asked users. It is a tool for finding real patterns and improving product decisions. AI products especially need user insight because trust, clarity, and usefulness are not always obvious from the builder's perspective alone. By the end of Day 3, Geniuses should feel that listening to users is not a soft extra. It is a real product skill that leads to stronger design, stronger trust, and stronger outcomes.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that one comment is not automatically an insight.
-   Encourage specific questions and specific changes.
-   Help Geniuses focus on repeated friction, trust gaps, and user confusion.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will use this insight to redesign their product flow and interface more intentionally.
