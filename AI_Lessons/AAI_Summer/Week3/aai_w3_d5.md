Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 3, Day 5 --- Redesign Studio, Product Critique, and User-Centered AI Showcase
========================================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

Day 5 is a flex day focused on redesign, critique, polish, and showcase. By this point, Geniuses have explored UI/UX design, culturally rooted product thinking, user insight, and intentional product flow. Today is about bringing all of that together into a stronger AI product experience. Geniuses will use studio-style work time to improve the parts of their product that matter most: clarity, flow, cultural grounding, trust, onboarding, usability, and responsiveness to user feedback.

During guided practice, the class will use a product critique framework to evaluate an AI interface and then apply targeted improvements to make it stronger. During independent practice, each Genius will improve their Week 3 mini-project with meaningful redesign choices and prepare for a short showcase. The lesson ends with critique, reflection, and presentation focused on how thoughtful design makes AI products more useful, more trustworthy, and more connected to real people.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Reflect on how AI product design improves through critique, feedback, and iteration.
-   Identify meaningful redesign choices that strengthen usability, trust, and relevance.
-   Improve an AI product by polishing layout, flow, language, and product guidance.
-   Use critique as a design tool for improving real digital products.
-   Strengthen an AI interface using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Present a redesigned AI product clearly in terms of audience, insight, and design decisions.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for viewing local web apps
-   Existing Week 3 project files
-   Optional critique worksheet or peer feedback form
-   Optional sticky notes for studio feedback rounds

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Improve a digital system by analyzing usability, flow, and feedback.
-   Computer Science Practices: Revise and strengthen a product through critique and iteration.
-   Web Development: Refine an interactive web product using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate whether a digital product is clear, inclusive, and trustworthy.
-   Career Readiness: Practice critique, communication, iteration, and presentation.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Improve a product by aligning it more closely with user needs and product value.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and frame the day around meaningful critique and strong redesign decisions.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Strongest Part or Weakest Part?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Strongest Part"** and the other side **"Needs Work."**
-   Read product situations aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches.
-   Example prompts:
    -   The page has a strong headline, but weak instructions
    -   The output is useful, but the next step is unclear
    -   The design is clean, but it still feels generic
    -   The trust note is strong, but the user flow is confusing
    -   The product is helpful, but the examples do not match the intended user
    -   The interface is readable, but the main button text is weak
    -   The product has great intent, but not enough user guidance
-   After each round, ask:
    -   Why is this a strength or weak point?
    -   What would you change first?
    -   What kind of user would feel this problem most?
    -   What is one redesign move that could improve it?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Fast Critique Round**

-   Show a simple product screen and ask Geniuses to quickly name:
    -   one strong choice
    -   one weak choice
    -   one redesign suggestion
-   Do this rapidly with 2--3 examples.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "Strong builders do not just make things. They look at what they made, critique it honestly, and improve what matters."
-   Ask:
    -   What part of your product still feels weakest?
    -   What part feels strongest and worth keeping?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 35 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand how to critique a product well and choose meaningful redesign priorities.
-   Use slides to guide the lesson and prepare Geniuses for studio work and showcase.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Day 5 Focus --- Critique, Refine, Showcase**
    -   Today is about polishing your design and explaining your choices clearly
-   **Slide 2: What Makes Critique Useful?**
    -   strong critique is specific
    -   strong critique focuses on the user experience
    -   strong critique leads to action
    -   critique should improve the product, not just judge it
-   **Slide 3: Weak Critique vs Strong Critique**
    -   Weak: "It looks weird"
    -   Strong: "The input section feels crowded, so the user may not know where to start"
    -   Strong critique points to a real experience problem
-   **Slide 4: Product Critique Categories**
    -   clarity
    -   flow
    -   trust
    -   cultural relevance
    -   user guidance
    -   usefulness of output
    -   visual hierarchy
-   **Slide 5: Questions Good Builders Ask**
    -   What does the user understand immediately?
    -   What might confuse them?
    -   Does this feel like it was made for the intended user?
    -   What supports trust?
    -   What should happen next after the output?
-   **Slide 6: Meaningful Redesign Priorities**
    -   improve the core experience first
    -   fix repeated friction points
    -   strengthen the most visible parts of the interface
    -   do not add random features that do not improve usability
-   **Slide 7: Product Polish Areas**
    -   stronger instructions
    -   cleaner spacing
    -   better labels
    -   more useful examples
    -   better result formatting
    -   clearer trust notes
    -   smoother mobile layout
-   **Slide 8: Showcase Thinking**
    -   Can you clearly explain:
        -   who the product is for?
        -   what user need it addresses?
        -   what changed through feedback?
        -   what redesign decisions made it stronger?
-   **Slide 9: What Strong Product Presentations Show**
    -   purpose
    -   audience
    -   user insight
    -   design logic
    -   improvement over time
-   **Slide 10: Finish Week 3 Strong**
    -   the goal is not perfection
    -   the goal is a more thoughtful, user-centered, grounded product

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes critique helpful instead of random?
-   Why is it important to redesign based on user need instead of just personal taste?
-   What makes a redesign meaningful?
-   What part of product polish matters most for trust?
-   How do you know when a product is stronger than it was before?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "What are the two most important redesign moves your product still needs before showcase?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 40 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses use a critique framework to evaluate a product and apply targeted redesign improvements.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is a redesign studio model they can reuse in future work.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Use an existing Week 3 project and apply a critique framework before editing code.

#### **Critique Framework**

Ask the class to review a sample AI product using these 5 prompts:

1.  What is clear right away?
2.  What might confuse the user?
3.  What helps or hurts trust?
4.  Does this feel relevant for the intended user?
5.  What one redesign would improve it most?

#### **Project Goal**

Take a product interface and make it stronger by improving:

-   clarity
-   trust
-   user guidance
-   flow
-   output presentation

#### **Sample Improvement Pattern**

The class will:

-   improve helper text
-   improve spacing and section hierarchy
-   improve the result card
-   add a better "what to do next" section
-   improve the trust note language

#### **Sample `index.html` Improvement**
```
<section class="flow-card">
  <div class="step-label">Before You Start</div>
  <h2>Get a better result</h2>
  <p class="helper-text">
    The clearer your input, the more useful your AI result will be. Include real tasks, deadlines, or responsibilities instead of one-word notes.
  </p>

  <div class="tips-box">
    <strong>Helpful tip:</strong>
    <p>Try including what is urgent, what has a deadline, and what feels most overwhelming right now.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section id="nextSection" class="flow-card hidden">
  <div class="step-label">Next Move</div>
  <h2>Use your result in real life</h2>
  <p id="followUpText">
    Choose one action to begin with today. Then decide whether you need to revise the plan based on your real time, energy, and priorities.
  </p>

  <div class="trust-note">
    <strong>Product Note:</strong> This tool is built to support your thinking, not control it. Use what helps, question what does not, and adapt the output to your real life.
  </div>
</section>
```
#### **Sample `style.css` Improvement**
```
.tips-box {
  margin-top: 14px;
  background: #fff7ed;
  border-left: 4px solid #f97316;
  border-radius: 12px;
  padding: 14px 16px;
  color: #7c2d12;
}

.tips-box p {
  margin-top: 6px;
}

.flow-card {
  transition: transform 0.2s ease, box-shadow 0.2s ease;
}

.flow-card:hover {
  transform: translateY(-2px);
  box-shadow: 0 14px 28px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.1);
}
```
#### **Sample `script.js` Improvement**
```
// script.js
// Applied AI Week 3 Day 5
// This script adds a stronger empty-state and clearer feedback flow.

const userInput = document.getElementById("userInput");
const generateButton = document.getElementById("generateButton");
const clearButton = document.getElementById("clearButton");
const statusMessage = document.getElementById("statusMessage");
const resultSection = document.getElementById("resultSection");
const nextSection = document.getElementById("nextSection");

const summaryOutput = document.getElementById("summaryOutput");
const actionsOutput = document.getElementById("actionsOutput");
const nextStepOutput = document.getElementById("nextStepOutput");

generateButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const text = userInput.value.trim();

  if (text === "") {
    statusMessage.textContent = "Start by typing a real set of notes, tasks, or responsibilities so the tool has something useful to work with.";
    resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
    nextSection.classList.add("hidden");
    return;
  }

  statusMessage.textContent = "Building your support plan...";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
  nextSection.classList.add("hidden");

  setTimeout(() => {
    summaryOutput.textContent = "Your input shows that you are trying to manage multiple priorities, so the best plan is one that lowers pressure and increases clarity.";
    actionsOutput.textContent = "1. Separate urgent responsibilities from flexible ones. 2. Choose one action you can finish today. 3. Group anything that can wait until later.";
    nextStepOutput.textContent = "Start with the one action that creates the most relief or momentum.";

    statusMessage.textContent = "Your result is ready.";
    resultSection.classList.remove("hidden");
    nextSection.classList.remove("hidden");
  }, 900);
});

clearButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  userInput.value = "";
  summaryOutput.textContent = "";
  actionsOutput.textContent = "";
  nextStepOutput.textContent = "";
  statusMessage.textContent = "";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
  nextSection.classList.add("hidden");
});
```
#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why critique should lead to specific redesign choices
-   Why support text and onboarding matter
-   Why small copy changes can improve trust and clarity
-   Why product polish is part of user-centered design
-   Why thoughtful simplification is often stronger than adding more

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   What specific redesign changes made the interface feel stronger?
-   Why did the helper text improve the experience?
-   What kind of user confusion does this redesign prevent?
-   How can critique make products better faster?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 65 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Give Geniuses time to redesign, polish, and prepare their product for showcase.
-   This completes the Week 3 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**AI Product Redesign --- Day 5 Studio Polish and Showcase Prep**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius chooses **2--3 meaningful redesign improvements** to apply to their product.

#### **Improvement Menu**

Geniuses can choose from the following:

-   improve helper text or onboarding
-   improve layout spacing and hierarchy
-   improve result card design
-   add a stronger trust note
-   add a better "what next" section
-   improve examples and placeholder text
-   improve mobile responsiveness
-   improve button labels
-   improve empty-state messaging
-   improve user flow sequence
-   improve the cultural grounding of language or examples
-   improve clarity based on user feedback
-   simplify cluttered sections
-   add a clearer user guidance box

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working `index.html`
-   A working `style.css`
-   A working `script.js`
-   At least 2 meaningful redesign improvements
-   A short product summary that includes:
    -   product name
    -   intended user or community
    -   user need addressed
    -   one major insight from the week
    -   two redesign improvements made today
    -   one way the product is stronger now
-   A 1--2 minute showcase or demo plan

#### **Showcase Prep Prompt**

Ask Geniuses to prepare a short share-out that answers:

-   What is your product called?
-   Who is it for?
-   What user need does it address?
-   What did you learn from feedback or insight this week?
-   What redesign changes made the product better?
-   What makes the product more trustworthy, usable, or relevant now?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses focus on redesign moves that actually improve the experience
-   Ask:
    -   What still feels weakest?
    -   What would help the user most?
    -   What change best reflects what you learned this week?
    -   What will make your showcase strongest?
-   Encourage specific design choices over random additions

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Celebrate product growth and reinforce that thoughtful design is a major part of AI product quality.
-   Bring the class together for a showcase.
-   Invite Geniuses to present individually or in small groups.
-   Each Genius should share:
    -   their product name
    -   their intended user or community
    -   one insight they learned
    -   two redesign improvements they made
    -   one reason the product is stronger now
-   After each share, invite quick audience feedback:
    -   What feels strongest?
    -   What feels more user-centered?
    -   What would be exciting to improve next?
-   Lead a final reflection discussion:
    -   What did you learn this week about AI product design?
    -   How did cultural grounding and user insight change your thinking?
    -   What part of redesign felt most important?
    -   What makes an AI product feel thoughtful instead of generic?

**Assessment:**
---------------

Student learning will be assessed through:

-   Participation in the icebreaker and discussion
-   Reflection responses during direct instruction
-   Completion of the guided critique and redesign build
-   Completion of at least 2 meaningful redesign improvements
-   Final showcase and product explanation
-   End-of-week reflection on design growth and user-centered thinking

### **Exit Ticket**

1.  What is one redesign choice that made your product stronger today?
2.  What is one insight from this week that changed your product?
3.  What makes your AI product more user-centered now than it was on Day 1?

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses feel that thoughtful design is a real form of building, not a cosmetic extra. Day 5 should feel like a redesign studio and a showcase of stronger product thinking. Across the week, Geniuses moved from usability basics into culturally rooted design, user insight, and product flow. By the end of Week 3, they should understand that great AI products are not just functional. They are clear, grounded, relevant, and designed with real people in mind.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in prompts, discussion, and examples.
-   Let flex time feel open, but keep Geniuses focused on meaningful redesign choices.
-   Encourage quality over quantity.
-   Help Geniuses explain not just what changed, but why it changed.
-   Reinforce that critique and redesign are core product skills.

**Week 3 Wrap-Up**
------------------

By the end of Week 3, each Genius should have:

-   A redesigned AI product interface
-   Stronger UI/UX choices
-   A more culturally rooted product direction
-   At least one user insight or feedback-based improvement
-   A clearer product flow
-   Better onboarding, guidance, or trust features
-   At least 2 meaningful redesign polish improvements from flex day
-   A short showcase or product explanation
