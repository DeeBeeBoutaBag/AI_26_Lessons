Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 3, Day 1 --- Introduction to UI/UX Design for AI Products
====================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

In Week 3, Geniuses shift from simply building AI products to designing AI products that feel clear, trustworthy, and useful for real people. Day 1 introduces UI and UX design in the context of Applied AI. Geniuses explore how interface design, user flow, layout, clarity, and trust signals shape the way people experience AI products. The lesson emphasizes that even a technically strong AI product can fail if the design is confusing, overwhelming, or disconnected from user needs.

During guided practice, Geniuses will examine an AI product interface, identify strengths and weaknesses, and then build a cleaner and more intentional UI in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. During independent practice, each Genius will begin redesigning part of their own Week 2 AI product with a stronger focus on usability, flow, clarity, and trust. This begins the Week 3 mini-project: improving an AI product through user-centered design.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Define UI and UX and explain how they affect AI product quality.
-   Identify what makes an AI product feel clear, confusing, trustworthy, or frustrating.
-   Analyze how layout, hierarchy, input flow, and feedback shape user experience.
-   Improve a web-based AI product interface using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Begin a redesign process for an existing AI product with stronger user-centered thinking.
-   Reflect on how design decisions affect trust, clarity, and product usefulness.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for viewing local web apps
-   Existing Week 2 project files
-   Optional screenshots of strong and weak product interfaces for critique

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Analyze how interface choices affect system use and user behavior.
-   Computer Science Practices: Test, critique, and improve a digital product through iteration.
-   Web Development: Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to improve interface structure and flow.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate product clarity, usability, and trust in digital experiences.
-   Career Readiness: Practice critique, empathy, reflection, and design communication.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Improve a product by making it more usable, understandable, and valuable to real users.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that a working AI product is not enough if the experience feels confusing or weak.
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Good Design or Bad Design?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the day.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Strong UX"** and the other side **"Weak UX."**
-   Read interface situations aloud and have Geniuses move quickly to the side that best matches.
-   Example prompts:
    -   A product has one clear button and one clear user task
    -   A product has five buttons and no clear starting point
    -   A product tells the user what the AI is doing while it loads
    -   A product gives a result with no title, no labels, and no next step
    -   A product has helpful instructions and examples before the user types
    -   A product uses tiny text and crowded spacing
    -   A product clearly explains what kind of output the user will receive
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What made this strong or weak?
    -   How would a user feel in this experience?
    -   Would this build trust?
    -   What would you improve first?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Fast Visual Critique**

-   Show two interface examples:
    -   one cluttered, unclear, or overwhelming
    -   one clean, structured, and easy to follow
-   Ask Geniuses to quickly identify:
    -   which one feels easier to use
    -   which one feels more trustworthy
    -   which one better supports an AI product
-   Let a few Geniuses explain why.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A product can have strong code and still feel weak to the user. This week is about making AI products feel better, clearer, and more human-centered."
-   Ask:
    -   What makes a digital product easy to use?
    -   What makes an AI product feel trustworthy from the first screen?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand the basics of UI/UX design and why those ideas matter deeply for AI products.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and pause often for discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Week 3 Focus --- Designing Better AI Products**
    -   This week is about usability, design, culture, and user insight
    -   Building is not enough if the product is hard to use
-   **Slide 2: What Is UI?**
    -   UI means User Interface
    -   It includes what users see and interact with:
        -   buttons
        -   text
        -   layout
        -   colors
        -   forms
        -   cards
        -   navigation
-   **Slide 3: What Is UX?**
    -   UX means User Experience
    -   It includes how the product feels to use:
        -   clarity
        -   ease
        -   flow
        -   trust
        -   feedback
        -   confusion or confidence
-   **Slide 4: Why UI/UX Matters in AI Products**
    -   AI products can already feel uncertain to users
    -   Bad design makes that worse
    -   Good design helps users understand:
        -   what to do
        -   what the AI is doing
        -   what the result means
        -   what to do next
-   **Slide 5: Common Problems in Weak AI Product Design**
    -   too much text
    -   unclear buttons
    -   bad spacing
    -   weak instructions
    -   confusing outputs
    -   no trust signals
    -   no feedback while loading
-   **Slide 6: Traits of Strong AI Product Design**
    -   clear starting point
    -   clear user goal
    -   readable layout
    -   strong visual hierarchy
    -   helpful labels
    -   clear outputs
    -   user guidance
    -   trust-building notes
-   **Slide 7: Visual Hierarchy**
    -   what should the user notice first?
    -   what matters most?
    -   what is secondary?
    -   how do size, spacing, and emphasis guide attention?
-   **Slide 8: User Flow**
    -   what does the user do first?
    -   what happens next?
    -   where might they get stuck?
    -   how does the product guide them?
-   **Slide 9: Trust in AI Interfaces**
    -   explain what the tool does
    -   give input examples
    -   use clear output labels
    -   show loading states
    -   avoid pretending certainty
    -   give next steps when possible
-   **Slide 10: Week 3 Mini-Project**
    -   redesign and improve an AI product interface using UX thinking and user-centered decisions

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes an AI product feel confusing?
-   Why does trust matter even before the AI gives a response?
-   What is one website or app that feels easy to use? Why?
-   What is one website or app that feels frustrating? Why?
-   How can design change whether a user stays or leaves?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "Describe a time you used an app, website, or tool that felt either really smooth or really frustrating. What made it feel that way?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 55 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses critique and improve an AI product interface using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is about redesigning for clarity, not just adding more features.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Use an existing Week 2 project or a new folder called `ai-product-redesign`.

#### **Project Goal**

Take a simple AI product demo and improve it so that it has:

-   stronger section hierarchy
-   clearer instructions
-   better spacing
-   a more readable output layout
-   better labels and feedback
-   a more intentional user flow

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open the project in VS Code.
2.  Use an existing Week 2 page or create:
    -   `index.html`
    -   `style.css`
    -   `script.js`
3.  Build a simple AI input/output interface with a redesign focus.

#### **Sample `script.js`**
```
// script.js
// Applied AI Week 3 Day 1
// This script simulates AI output while focusing on product flow and UI/UX clarity.

const userInput = document.getElementById("userInput");
const generateButton = document.getElementById("generateButton");
const clearButton = document.getElementById("clearButton");
const statusMessage = document.getElementById("statusMessage");
const resultSection = document.getElementById("resultSection");

const summaryOutput = document.getElementById("summaryOutput");
const actionsOutput = document.getElementById("actionsOutput");
const nextStepOutput = document.getElementById("nextStepOutput");

generateButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const text = userInput.value.trim();

  if (text === "") {
    statusMessage.textContent = "Please enter your notes first.";
    resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
    return;
  }

  statusMessage.textContent = "Organizing your notes...";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");

  setTimeout(() => {
    summaryOutput.textContent = "Your notes include several tasks that need coordination and follow-through.";
    actionsOutput.textContent = "1. Finalize the flyer. 2. Confirm snack responsibilities. 3. Send the sign-up form by Thursday.";
    nextStepOutput.textContent = "Start by assigning one person to send the sign-up form so the group has a clear deadline owner.";

    statusMessage.textContent = "Done.";
    resultSection.classList.remove("hidden");
  }, 800);
});

clearButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  userInput.value = "";
  summaryOutput.textContent = "";
  actionsOutput.textContent = "";
  nextStepOutput.textContent = "";
  statusMessage.textContent = "";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
});
```

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   What changed when the layout became cleaner?
-   Which part of the redesign helped the user most?
-   What still feels weak or incomplete?
-   How does this design help users trust the tool more?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently begin redesigning their own AI product with better UI/UX decisions.
-   This begins the Week 3 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**AI Product Redesign --- Day 1 UI/UX Foundation**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius chooses an AI product they built previously or creates a simple AI product concept and improves the interface design.

They must redesign the product so it includes:

-   a clearer title and purpose
-   improved layout and spacing
-   stronger labels or instructions
-   a more readable output area
-   one trust-building feature
-   one improvement to user flow

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working `index.html`
-   A working `style.css`
-   A working `script.js`
-   A redesigned interface with:
    -   product name
    -   clear purpose
    -   input area
    -   output area
    -   one trust note or guidance message
    -   one improved user flow decision
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What part of your interface did you improve most?
    -   How does your redesign help the user better?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Encourage Geniuses to improve usability before adding complexity
-   Ask:
    -   What does the user see first?
    -   What should feel most important on the screen?
    -   What might confuse the user?
    -   Does the page clearly explain what the AI does?
-   Help Geniuses reduce clutter and improve clarity

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that UI/UX is a major part of AI product quality and user trust.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to show:
    -   their product name
    -   one design change they made
    -   one trust-building feature they added
    -   one reason the product feels better now
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a product easier to use?
    -   What makes a user trust an AI tool?
    -   How can design improve a product even when the AI stays the same?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will explore culturally rooted design and how AI products should reflect real communities, values, and lived experience.

**Assessment:**
---------------

Student learning will be assessed through:

-   Participation in the icebreaker and discussion
-   Reflection responses during direct instruction
-   Completion of the guided UI/UX redesign build
-   Completion of an independent product redesign
-   Final share-out and explanation of design improvements

### **Exit Ticket**

1.  What is the difference between UI and UX?
2.  What is one design choice that can improve trust in an AI product?
3.  What change made your product easier to use today?

---------------------

    -   add helper text beneath the input box

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses understand that great AI products are not judged only by their technical power. They are also judged by how they feel to use. Day 1 of Week 3 introduces a major mindset shift: product quality includes usability, trust, clarity, and visual communication. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel that interface and experience design are not extra details. They are part of what makes an AI product truly strong.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that design is not decoration. It shapes understanding and trust.
-   Encourage Geniuses to simplify rather than overcrowd.
-   Help Geniuses think about what the user sees, feels, and understands at each step.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow the focus will move from general UX into culturally rooted design.

-   Better labels and instructions
