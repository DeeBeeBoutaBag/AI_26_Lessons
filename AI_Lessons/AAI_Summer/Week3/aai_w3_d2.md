Lesson Plan: Applied AI Week 3, Day 2 --- Culturally Rooted Design for AI Products
================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

On Day 2, Geniuses explore what it means to design AI products that are not just functional and visually strong, but also culturally rooted, relevant, and respectful of the people they serve. The lesson centers on the idea that strong products should reflect real communities, lived experiences, language, values, and everyday realities. Geniuses examine how design choices, language choices, examples, assumptions, and workflows can either make users feel seen or make them feel excluded.

During guided practice, the class redesigns an AI product interface so it feels more grounded in a specific community and user context. Geniuses use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to improve interface language, examples, onboarding, trust notes, and user flow so the product reflects real people more intentionally. During independent practice, each Genius updates their Week 3 mini-project by choosing a specific user community and redesigning their AI product to better serve that group with greater care, clarity, and cultural relevance.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Explain what culturally rooted design means in the context of AI products.
-   Identify ways AI products can unintentionally feel generic, disconnected, or exclusionary.
-   Analyze how language, examples, visuals, and product assumptions affect whether users feel seen and respected.
-   Improve an AI product by designing for a specific community, context, or lived experience.
-   Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to redesign interface content and user flow with stronger cultural grounding.
-   Reflect on how culturally rooted design improves trust, usability, and product relevance.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Browser for viewing local web apps
-   Existing Week 3 Day 1 project files
-   Optional examples of products that feel generic versus products that feel community-aware

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Analyze how system and interface choices affect different users and communities.
-   Computer Science Practices: Critique and improve digital tools based on real user context and lived experience.
-   Web Development: Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to redesign a product for stronger user connection and clarity.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate whether a digital product is inclusive, relevant, and respectful.
-   Career Readiness: Practice empathy, critique, reflection, and human-centered design thinking.
-   Entrepreneurship and Innovation: Design products that create value by understanding and respecting real communities.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that strong AI products should feel connected to real people, not built for a vague "everyone."
-   Start with an icebreaker called **"Who Is This Really For?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label different parts of the room with user groups such as:
    -   **High School Students**
    -   **Families**
    -   **Elders**
    -   **Small Business Owners**
    -   **Creators**
    -   **Community Organizations**
-   Read product descriptions aloud and have Geniuses move to the group they think the product is actually designed for.
-   Example prompts:
    -   An AI assistant that explains confusing school emails
    -   An AI tool that turns meeting notes into action plans
    -   An AI chatbot that helps first-time job seekers practice interviews
    -   An AI planner that reminds caregivers about tasks and appointments
    -   An AI tool that rewrites formal language into simpler language
    -   An AI website that helps local businesses write social media posts
-   After each round, ask:
    -   Who is this really for?
    -   What clues tell you that?
    -   What would make this feel more culturally grounded?
    -   What would make this feel more generic or disconnected?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Rapid Product Critique**

-   Show short product taglines or interface snippets and ask Geniuses:
    -   Who does this seem designed for?
    -   Who might feel left out?
    -   What assumptions is this product making?
-   Let Geniuses quickly discuss in pairs and share out.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A lot of products say they are for everyone, but the truth is that strong products usually serve specific people in specific contexts really well."
-   Ask:
    -   What makes a product feel like it understands you?
    -   What makes a product feel generic or out of touch?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand culturally rooted design and why it matters for AI products, user trust, and real-world usefulness.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and stop often for discussion.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Day 2 Focus --- Designing for Real Communities**
    -   Today is about making AI products feel grounded in real people, not generic users.
-   **Slide 2: What Is Culturally Rooted Design?**
    -   Designing with awareness of:
        -   community context
        -   language
        -   values
        -   lived experience
        -   identity
        -   trust
        -   access
    -   It means building with people in mind, not just around them.
-   **Slide 3: Why This Matters in AI Products**
    -   AI tools already carry assumptions
    -   If the product design is generic, users may feel unseen, misunderstood, or excluded
    -   Strong design makes users feel respected and understood
-   **Slide 4: Signs of a Generic Product**
    -   vague user identity
    -   generic examples
    -   formal or distant language
    -   no real awareness of the user's world
    -   one-size-fits-all design choices
-   **Slide 5: Signs of a Culturally Rooted Product**
    -   clear user community
    -   relevant examples
    -   language that matches the audience
    -   features shaped by real needs
    -   awareness of context, access, and trust
-   **Slide 6: Design Choices That Affect Belonging**
    -   headlines
    -   placeholder text
    -   examples
    -   tone
    -   colors and visuals
    -   trust notes
    -   onboarding flow
-   **Slide 7: Questions Builders Should Ask**
    -   Who is this really for?
    -   What assumptions am I making about the user?
    -   Does this language sound natural for this audience?
    -   Would this user feel seen here?
    -   What might this product be missing because of my own perspective?
-   **Slide 8: Culturally Rooted Does Not Mean Stereotyping**
    -   The goal is not caricature
    -   The goal is relevance, respect, and specificity
    -   Design should be informed by real insight, not lazy assumptions
-   **Slide 9: AI Products and Community Trust**
    -   community-aware products build stronger trust
    -   trust matters even more when AI is involved
    -   people need to understand that the product respects their reality
-   **Slide 10: Week 3 Mini-Project Growth**
    -   Day 1: improve clarity and UX
    -   Day 2: ground the product in a real user community

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes a product feel like it understands its users?
-   Why do generic products often feel weaker?
-   How can language choices affect whether users trust a product?
-   What is the difference between culturally rooted design and stereotyping?
-   Why should AI products be especially careful about cultural relevance?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "Describe a product, service, or app that feels like it was really made for a certain community. What makes it feel that way?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 55 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses redesign an AI product interface so it feels more grounded in a specific user group, community, or real-life context.
-   Explain that today's guided practice is not mainly about adding features. It is about making the product feel more relevant and more connected to real users.

#### **Sample `script.js`**
```
// script.js
// Applied AI Week 3 Day 2
// This script simulates AI support while focusing on culturally rooted design
// and user-centered messaging.

const userInput = document.getElementById("userInput");
const generateButton = document.getElementById("generateButton");
const clearButton = document.getElementById("clearButton");
const statusMessage = document.getElementById("statusMessage");
const resultSection = document.getElementById("resultSection");

const summaryOutput = document.getElementById("summaryOutput");
const actionsOutput = document.getElementById("actionsOutput");
const nextStepOutput = document.getElementById("nextStepOutput");

generateButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  const text = userInput.value.trim();

  if (text === "") {
    statusMessage.textContent = "Type in what you have going on first.";
    resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
    return;
  }

  statusMessage.textContent = "Getting your plan together...";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");

  setTimeout(() => {
    summaryOutput.textContent = "You have multiple responsibilities competing for your time, so the goal is to reduce pressure and create a manageable order.";
    actionsOutput.textContent = "1. Identify the most urgent school deadline. 2. Put time aside for the family responsibility you mentioned. 3. Break your presentation into one smaller step you can finish today.";
    nextStepOutput.textContent = "Start with the assignment or task that has the closest deadline so you can lower stress and create more room for everything else.";

    statusMessage.textContent = "Your plan is ready.";
    resultSection.classList.remove("hidden");
  }, 800);
});

clearButton.addEventListener("click", () => {
  userInput.value = "";
  summaryOutput.textContent = "";
  actionsOutput.textContent = "";
  nextStepOutput.textContent = "";
  statusMessage.textContent = "";
  resultSection.classList.add("hidden");
});
```

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently redesign their AI product for a specific community, context, or user group.
-   This continues the Week 3 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**AI Product Redesign --- Day 2 Culturally Rooted Product Design**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius chooses a specific user group or community for their product and redesigns the interface so it feels more relevant and more community-aware.

They must improve:

-   headline or product description
-   example text or placeholder content
-   instructions and user guidance
-   trust note or product note
-   at least one user flow choice based on that community's real needs

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working `index.html`
-   A working `style.css`
-   A working `script.js`
-   A redesigned interface with:
    -   a clearly defined user community
    -   more specific language
    -   relevant example content
    -   a trust-building note
    -   one stronger community-aware design choice
-   A short written reflection:
    -   Who is your product really for?
    -   What did you change so the product feels more rooted in that community?
    -   How does that make the product stronger?

#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Push Geniuses to choose a real user group, not "everyone"
-   Ask:
    -   What language would feel natural for this audience?
    -   What examples would actually make sense here?
    -   What might this user need that a generic product would miss?
    -   Does this design feel respectful and grounded?
-   Help Geniuses avoid stereotypes and focus on real relevance

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that strong AI products should feel connected to real people, communities, and lived experiences.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to show:
    -   their product name
    -   the community they designed for
    -   one language or example change they made
    -   one trust-building design choice they added
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a product feel culturally grounded?
    -   Why is generic design often weaker?
    -   How can a builder design with respect and relevance?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will explore user insight, interviews, and feedback so design decisions can be shaped by real user voices.

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses realize that culturally rooted design is not a side detail or an extra layer added at the end. It is a core part of making products relevant, respectful, and real. AI products can easily become generic or disconnected if builders do not think carefully about community context, language, and lived experience. By the end of Day 2, Geniuses should understand that stronger products are not just more polished. They are more grounded in the people they are meant to serve.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that culturally rooted design is about relevance and respect, not stereotype or assumption.
-   Encourage specificity without exaggeration.
-   Help Geniuses think deeply about user language, examples, and trust.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will gather and apply user insight to improve their product further.
