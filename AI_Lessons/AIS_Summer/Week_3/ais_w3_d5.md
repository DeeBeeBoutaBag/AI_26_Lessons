Lesson Plan: AI Systems Week 3, Day 5 --- Vision AI Studio, Sports Analytics Critique, and Computer Vision Software Showcase
==========================================================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

Day 5 brings together everything Geniuses explored in Week 3: computer vision foundations, object detection, sports analytics, and video vision workflows. Today is a studio day focused on refinement, critique, and showcase. Geniuses use work time to strengthen a **vision-based AI software concept** so it is technically clearer, more useful, and more responsible. The lesson emphasizes that strong vision AI products are not just exciting because they "see." They must also be designed around real user value, realistic outputs, and careful trust thinking.

Geniuses will refine their concept so it clearly explains what visual input the system uses, what the system detects or tracks, what insight or output it produces, who it helps, and what risks must be managed. During guided practice, the class builds a Python-based concept summarizer for a vision AI software system that organizes product purpose, technical behavior, and responsible design choices. During independent practice, each Genius finalizes a vision AI software concept and prepares a short showcase. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel that they are not only learning vision AI ideas. They are learning how to design real software systems powered by computer vision.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Synthesize key ideas from Week 3 on computer vision, object detection, tracking, sports analytics, and video workflows.
-   Refine a vision AI software concept so it is clearer technically and more product-ready.
-   Explain why computer vision is the correct AI system type for a chosen use case.
-   Identify trust, fairness, privacy, and review needs in a visual AI product.
-   Build a Python-based concept summarizer for a vision AI system.
-   Present a vision AI software concept clearly in terms of inputs, tasks, outputs, and responsible design.
-   Prepare for deeper AI Systems work in the coming weeks.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Python installed and working
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Terminal access in VS Code
-   Existing Week 3 files and concept work
-   Starter folder for Python files

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Connect system behavior, visual inputs, outputs, and user goals into a coherent software concept.
-   Computer Science Practices: Build and refine a vision AI workflow with clearer product structure and stronger technical explanation.
-   Programming: Use Python to support structured system planning and communication.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate the usefulness, trustworthiness, and risks of computer vision products.
-   Career Readiness: Practice technical communication, critique, and presentation.
-   Technical Foundations: Explain the role of vision systems in modern AI software products.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and frame the day around refining vision AI concepts into stronger software ideas.
-   Start with a fun icebreaker called **"Cool Vision or Useful Vision?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Cool"** and the other side **"Useful."**
-   Read vision AI ideas aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches how the idea feels.
-   Example prompts:
    -   A system that detects whether a photo has a cat
    -   A system that tracks player spacing for coaches
    -   A system that puts funny labels on random objects in a room
    -   A system that helps blind or low-vision users understand a scene
    -   A system that auto-generates sports highlights
    -   A system that watches students constantly with no clear purpose
    -   A system that tracks products on shelves for inventory alerts
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What makes this mostly cool or actually useful?
    -   Who is it helping?
    -   What problem does it solve?
    -   What trust issue matters here?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Product Critique Filter**

-   Put vision AI product ideas on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to sort them into:
    -   useful and realistic
    -   interesting but weak
    -   powerful but needs strict guardrails
-   Discuss how vision AI becomes stronger when it is tied to real users and real tasks.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "This week was not just about teaching computers to see. It was about understanding what useful vision systems actually do for people."
-   Ask:
    -   What makes a vision system worth building?
    -   What makes a vision system trustworthy enough to use?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 40 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses review the core ideas from the week and prepare to refine and present their software concepts.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and keep it interactive.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Week 3 Wrap-Up --- AI Systems That See**
    -   This week focused on systems that classify, detect, track, and interpret images and video
    -   Geniuses moved from broad vision concepts into real-world product domains like sports analytics
-   **Slide 2: What Makes a Vision AI Software Concept Strong?**
    -   clear visual input
    -   clear system task
    -   useful output
    -   real user
    -   real product value
    -   responsible design choices
-   **Slide 3: Weak Vision AI Concepts**
    -   vague use case
    -   no real user
    -   no reason vision is needed
    -   no trust thinking
    -   flashy idea with no useful output
-   **Slide 4: Strong Vision AI Concepts**
    -   specific scene or video input
    -   clear task like classification, detection, tracking, or event recognition
    -   useful output for a real user
    -   realistic limitations
    -   review and trust thinking built in
-   **Slide 5: Week 3 System Types Reviewed**
    -   image classification
    -   object detection
    -   player and object tracking
    -   event detection
    -   sports analytics
    -   video vision
-   **Slide 6: Why Vision Is the Right System Type**
    -   when the problem depends on images or video
    -   when visual patterns matter
    -   when location, movement, or scene details matter
    -   when text alone cannot solve the task
-   **Slide 7: Trust and Guardrails in Vision AI**
    -   review before major decisions
    -   avoid surveillance misuse
    -   be careful with fairness and bias
    -   communicate uncertainty
    -   use human judgment in sensitive contexts
-   **Slide 8: Product Questions Builders Should Answer**
    -   What visual input does the system use?
    -   What is the system trying to see or detect?
    -   What output helps the user?
    -   What could go wrong?
    -   What should the human still review?
-   **Slide 9: Showcase Thinking**
    -   Can you clearly explain:
        -   the system name?
        -   the user?
        -   the visual input?
        -   the task?
        -   the output?
        -   the trust concern?
        -   the responsible design choice?
-   **Slide 10: Looking Ahead**
    -   Week 4 will move into the next major AI Systems theme
    -   The vision ideas from this week can feed directly into larger final project ideas

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes a vision AI concept useful instead of only impressive?
-   Why does a clear user matter in vision software?
-   What trust issue matters most in visual AI systems?
-   How do you know when computer vision is the right system behavior?
-   What makes a vision app concept feel real and product-ready?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "What is the strongest part of your vision AI system concept right now, and what still needs improvement?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 45 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses build a Python-based concept summarizer that organizes a vision AI software idea clearly.
-   Explain that today's code-along is about communicating a system idea with technical and responsible clarity.
-   The class will create `vision_system_summary.py`.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Create a folder called `vision-system-summary`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a Python program that:

-   asks the user key questions about a vision AI concept
-   organizes the answers into a structured summary
-   helps Geniuses explain the concept clearly

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open VS Code.
2.  Create a folder called `vision-system-summary`.
3.  Create a file called `vision_system_summary.py`.
4.  Run the file in the terminal.

#### **Sample Code**
```
# vision_system_summary.py
# AI Systems Week 3 Day 5
# This program helps Geniuses summarize a vision AI software concept.

print("Welcome to the Vision System Summary Tool\n")

system_name = input("What is the name of your vision AI system? ")
user = input("Who is the system for? ")
visual_input = input("What visual input does the system use? ")
task_type = input("What task does the system perform? ")
output_type = input("What useful output does the system produce? ")
trust_concern = input("What is one trust or ethics concern? ")
responsible_design = input("What is one responsible design choice? ")

print("\n--- Vision AI Software Summary ---")
print(f"System Name: {system_name}")
print(f"User: {user}")
print(f"Visual Input: {visual_input}")
print(f"Task Type: {task_type}")
print(f"Output Type: {output_type}")
print(f"Trust Concern: {trust_concern}")
print(f"Responsible Design Choice: {responsible_design}")

print("\n--- Reflection ---")
print("Why is vision the right AI system type here?")
print("What should the user still verify before trusting the output?")
print("What makes this system more useful than just visually impressive?")
```
#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why vision system concepts need clear technical structure
-   Why user value matters as much as the detection or tracking idea
-   Why trust concerns should be named directly
-   Why summary tools help builders think more clearly
-   Why a strong concept is a bridge between technical learning and real software design

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   What section of the summary feels most important?
-   What makes a vision AI use case feel real?
-   Why should responsible design be included in the summary, not added later?
-   How would this summary help a teammate, judge, or investor understand the product?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 45 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently refine a vision AI software concept and prepare for a short showcase.
-   This completes the Week 3 mini-project.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**Week 3 Vision AI Software Concept --- Showcase Build**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius finalizes a vision AI software concept that uses one or more of the following:

-   image classification
-   object detection
-   tracking
-   sports analytics
-   video vision
-   event detection

Their concept must clearly explain:

-   system name
-   user
-   visual input
-   task type
-   useful output
-   why vision is the correct AI system behavior
-   one trust or ethics concern
-   one responsible design choice

They may use Python to print the summary in a clear format.

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working Python file called `week3_vision_system_showcase.py`
-   A complete vision system concept with:
    -   system name
    -   user
    -   visual input
    -   task type
    -   output
    -   why vision fits
    -   one trust concern
    -   one responsible design choice
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What kind of vision AI system is this?
    -   Why would a user want it?
    -   What part of the output should never be trusted blindly?

#### **Independent Practice Starter Code**
```
# week3_vision_system_showcase.py
# Week 3 independent practice
# This program prints a refined vision AI software concept.

print("Week 3 Vision AI Software Concept\n")

system_name = "GameSense AI"
user = "Basketball coaches and player development staff"
visual_input = "Practice and game video footage"
task_type = "Object detection, player tracking, and event detection"
output_type = "Movement summaries, spacing insights, and key event markers"
why_vision_fits = "The system depends on understanding position, motion, and events in visual footage, which cannot be captured well through text alone"
trust_concern = "The system could overemphasize visible movement while missing game context, intent, and off-camera information"
responsible_design = "The output should support film study and coaching discussion, not replace human expertise or become the only measure of player value"

print(f"System Name: {system_name}")
print(f"User: {user}")
print(f"Visual Input: {visual_input}")
print(f"Task Type: {task_type}")
print(f"Output Type: {output_type}")
print(f"Why Vision Fits: {why_vision_fits}")
print(f"Trust Concern: {trust_concern}")
print(f"Responsible Design Choice: {responsible_design}")
```
#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses focus on clarity, usefulness, and realism
-   Ask:
    -   What exactly does the system see?
    -   What does the system help the user understand?
    -   Why is vision necessary here?
    -   What is the most important trust issue?
-   Encourage concepts that are specific, modern, and grounded in real value

### 5. **Closure (Time: 30 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Celebrate the week's learning and reinforce that vision AI systems can be useful, powerful, and responsibly designed.
-   Bring the class together for a Week 3 showcase.
-   Ask 5--7 Geniuses to share:
    -   their system name
    -   what visual input it uses
    -   what it detects, tracks, or analyzes
    -   who it is for
    -   one trust concern
    -   one responsible design choice
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What did you learn this week about computer vision systems?
    -   What is the difference between classification, detection, tracking, and event recognition?
    -   What makes a vision AI software concept feel real and useful?
    -   Why is trust central in systems that analyze visual information?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Week 4 will move into the next major AI Systems theme and build on the idea that modern AI products often combine seeing, generating, and deciding in larger software systems.

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses realize that vision AI is not only about technical detection. It is about designing software systems that turn visual input into useful decisions, insights, and support for real users. Day 5 helps bring Week 3 together by moving from separate labs into a stronger product concept that someone else could understand and build on. By the end of the week, Geniuses should feel more confident explaining what vision AI systems do, how they are structured, and why they need thoughtful trust and review design.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Reinforce that strong vision systems solve real problems for real users.
-   Encourage concepts that are useful, not only visually impressive.
-   Help Geniuses speak clearly about fairness, trust, privacy, and review.
-   Use the showcase to see who is beginning to think like a real AI systems builder.
