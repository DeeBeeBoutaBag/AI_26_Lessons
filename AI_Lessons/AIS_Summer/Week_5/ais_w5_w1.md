Lesson Plan: AI Systems Week 5, Day 1 --- Capstone Vision, Tech Principles, and Product Readiness
===============================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

Week 5 is the final guided week before Geniuses move into their capstone build phase. Day 1 is about turning everything they have explored in AI Systems into a **serious capstone-ready software concept**. After studying AI foundations, generation, vision, and multimodal agents, Geniuses now focus on product readiness: what they are building, who it is for, why it matters, and what principles should guide the build.

This lesson introduces the idea that strong AI software is not only defined by features. It is also defined by **clear purpose, strong technical principles, responsible design values, and realistic product thinking**. Geniuses will define the problem, user, AI system type, and product vision for their capstone while also creating a set of **tech principles** that will guide how they build. During guided practice, the class builds a Python-based capstone concept and principles organizer. During independent practice, each Genius creates the first official version of their capstone software concept and a set of technical product principles. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel that their capstone is no longer just an idea. It is becoming a product with direction, standards, and purpose.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Define a capstone AI software concept clearly in terms of user, problem, and system behavior.
-   Explain what makes an AI app worth building as a real product.
-   Identify strong technical and product principles for an AI software system.
-   Distinguish between a cool feature idea and a capstone-ready product concept.
-   Build a Python-based capstone concept organizer in VS Code.
-   Create a first version of a capstone vision and tech principles document.
-   Reflect on how strong principles improve the quality, trust, and clarity of a software build.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Python installed and working
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Terminal access in VS Code
-   Existing AI Systems concept work from Weeks 1--4
-   Starter folder for Python files
-   Markdown preview in VS Code if writing docs alongside code

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Organize product concepts into systems, users, goals, and constraints.
-   Computer Science Practices: Define a software concept with clear technical behavior and user value.
-   Technical Communication: Explain a system clearly in terms of purpose, inputs, outputs, and design principles.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate whether an AI product is useful, responsible, and trustworthy.
-   Career Readiness: Practice product thinking, technical planning, and principled design.
-   Technical Foundations: Connect AI system capabilities to real software product direction.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that strong capstones need more than technical features. They need a clear product and a clear set of values.
-   Start with a fun icebreaker called **"Cool Build or Real Product?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Cool Build"** and the other side **"Real Product."**
-   Read AI software ideas aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches how the idea currently feels.
-   Example prompts:
    -   A chatbot that answers random questions
    -   A worksheet explainer agent for Geniuses
    -   A sports analytics app for coaches
    -   An agent that does everything for everyone
    -   A visual event planning assistant
    -   A voice-enabled scene explainer for accessibility support
    -   An image generator with no real audience
    -   A training feedback assistant for athletes
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What makes this feel like a real product?
    -   What is missing when it only feels like a cool build?
    -   Who is the user?
    -   What problem is being solved?
    -   What principle or value would make the build stronger?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Product Filter**

-   Put project ideas on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to sort them into:
    -   interesting but unclear
    -   useful and product-ready
    -   powerful but needs tighter scope
-   Discuss how user clarity, problem clarity, and trust design matter.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "This week is about getting your capstone ready to build for real. That means clarity, direction, and principles."
-   Ask:
    -   What makes an AI app worth building?
    -   What should guide the build besides just what is technically possible?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand what makes a capstone concept strong and why tech principles matter in AI software.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and keep it interactive.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Week 5 Focus --- Capstone Launch**
    -   This week prepares Geniuses for the final capstone build
    -   Today is about defining the product clearly and choosing the principles that guide the build
-   **Slide 2: What Makes a Strong Capstone Product?**
    -   clear user
    -   clear problem
    -   clear AI system behavior
    -   real value
    -   realistic scope
    -   trust and responsibility thinking
-   **Slide 3: Product Vision**
    -   What is the product?
    -   Who is it for?
    -   What does it help them do?
    -   Why does it matter?
    -   Why is AI useful here?
-   **Slide 4: Product-Ready vs Feature-Only Thinking**
    -   Feature-only thinking:
        -   "it can generate images"
        -   "it can talk"
        -   "it can detect objects"
    -   Product thinking:
        -   "it helps this user solve this problem using these capabilities"
-   **Slide 5: What Are Tech Principles?**
    -   Tech principles are the rules and values that guide how the product should be built
    -   They answer:
        -   what should this system prioritize?
        -   what should this system avoid?
        -   how should this system behave?
-   **Slide 6: Example Tech Principles**
    -   build for real user value
    -   keep outputs understandable
    -   support human review
    -   do not overclaim what the AI knows
    -   prioritize accessibility
    -   use AI only where it adds meaningful value
    -   keep trust and clarity visible
    -   respect privacy and data boundaries
-   **Slide 7: Why Principles Matter**
    -   they shape product decisions
    -   they help teams stay focused
    -   they improve trust and usability
    -   they keep the capstone from becoming random or overbuilt
-   **Slide 8: Capstone Questions Builders Should Answer**
    -   Who is the user?
    -   What is the exact problem?
    -   What AI system behavior is being used?
    -   What should the product do first?
    -   What values should guide the build?
-   **Slide 9: Weak vs Strong Capstone Direction**
    -   Weak:
        -   vague user
        -   too many features
        -   no real problem
        -   no trust thinking
    -   Strong:
        -   clear scope
        -   clear user
        -   useful AI behavior
        -   strong product principles
        -   realistic next steps
-   **Slide 10: Week 5 Direction**
    -   Geniuses will use this week to create the full capstone launch package: concept, docs, trust policies, prototype plan, and pitch

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes an AI app feel like a real product?
-   Why is user clarity more important than feature count?
-   Why do tech principles matter in AI software?
-   What principles should almost every AI app include?
-   How can principles make a capstone stronger during the build phase?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "What is your capstone trying to help someone do, and what principles should guide how it is built?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses build a Python-based capstone concept and principles organizer.
-   Explain that today's code-along is about organizing capstone thinking clearly and professionally.
-   The class will create `capstone_vision_builder.py`.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Create a folder called `capstone-vision-builder`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a Python program that:

-   asks the user for the capstone name
-   asks who the user is
-   asks what problem is being solved
-   asks what AI system behavior is being used
-   asks for three tech principles
-   prints a structured capstone summary

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open VS Code.
2.  Create a folder called `capstone-vision-builder`.
3.  Create a file called `capstone_vision_builder.py`.
4.  Run the file in the terminal.

#### **Sample Code**
```
# capstone_vision_builder.py
# AI Systems Week 5 Day 1
# This program helps Geniuses define a capstone product vision
# and the principles that should guide the build.

print("Welcome to the Capstone Vision Builder\n")

project_name = input("What is the name of your capstone project? ")
user = input("Who is the product for? ")
problem = input("What problem does it solve? ")
ai_behavior = input("What AI system behavior does it use? ")
principle_1 = input("Enter tech principle 1: ")
principle_2 = input("Enter tech principle 2: ")
principle_3 = input("Enter tech principle 3: ")

print("\n--- Capstone Vision Summary ---")
print(f"Project Name: {project_name}")
print(f"User: {user}")
print(f"Problem: {problem}")
print(f"AI System Behavior: {ai_behavior}")

print("\n--- Tech Principles ---")
print(f"1. {principle_1}")\
print(f"2. {principle_2}")\
print(f"3. {principle_3}")

print("\n--- Reflection ---")
print("Why is this product worth building?")
print("What makes the AI useful here?")
print("How will these principles shape the build?")
```
#### **Optional Extension**

Have Geniuses generate a cleaner principles list with the API later in the week, but Day 1 should prioritize their own thinking first.

#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why product vision should stay simple and clear
-   Why the user and problem must be specific
-   Why AI behavior should be named directly
-   Why principles help make the build more focused
-   Why writing things clearly is part of technical maturity

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   Which part of the capstone vision felt easiest?
-   Which part felt hardest?
-   What makes a principle strong instead of generic?
-   How would these principles help during a stressful build week?

### 4. **Independent Practice (Time: 35 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Geniuses independently create the first official version of their capstone product vision and tech principles.
-   This begins the Week 5 capstone launch package.

#### **Mini-Project Title**

**Capstone Launch Package --- Day 1 Product Vision and Tech Principles**

#### **Independent Task**

Each Genius creates a Python file and a short written concept for their capstone.

Their work must include:

-   project name
-   user
-   problem
-   AI system behavior
-   why the product matters
-   at least 4 tech principles

Examples of tech principles:

-   Keep the product useful before making it flashy
-   Support human review for important outputs
-   Use AI only where it adds meaningful value
-   Keep outputs understandable for users
-   Design for trust, not just speed
-   Protect user privacy and avoid unnecessary data use

#### **Required Deliverables**

-   A working Python file called `my_capstone_vision.py`
-   A written capstone concept summary
-   A set of at least 4 tech principles
-   A capstone vision that includes:
    -   project name
    -   user
    -   problem
    -   AI system behavior
    -   why it matters
    -   principles for the build
-   A short written reflection:
    -   What makes this capstone worth building?
    -   What principle matters most to your product?
    -   What part of the capstone still needs clarification?

#### **Independent Practice Starter Code**
```
# my_capstone_vision.py
# Week 5 Day 1 independent practice
# This program prints a capstone product vision and tech principles.

print("My Capstone Product Vision\n")

project_name = "CourtSense AI"
user = "Basketball coaches and athletes"
problem = "Practice footage is hard to review quickly and consistently without extra support"
ai_behavior = "Computer vision, tracking, and multimodal agent feedback"
why_it_matters = "The system helps users turn visual sports data into clearer coaching and training insight"

principles = [
    "Build for real training value, not just flashy analytics",
    "Use AI to support coaching judgment, not replace it",
    "Keep outputs understandable and actionable",
    "Make trust and review part of the workflow"
]

print(f"Project Name: {project_name}")
print(f"User: {user}")
print(f"Problem: {problem}")
print(f"AI System Behavior: {ai_behavior}")
print(f"Why It Matters: {why_it_matters}")

print("\n--- Tech Principles ---")
for principle in principles:
    print(f"- {principle}")
```
#### **Facilitator Support Moves**

-   Help Geniuses narrow their product if it feels too broad
-   Ask:
    -   Who exactly is the user?
    -   What is the one main problem?
    -   What AI system behavior is doing the real work?
    -   Which principle should shape every decision during the build?
-   Encourage specificity and realism over overbuilt feature lists

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that strong capstones begin with product clarity and principled design.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to share:
    -   their capstone name
    -   who it is for
    -   the problem it solves
    -   one tech principle they care about most
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a capstone product-ready?
    -   Why are principles useful before coding more deeply?
    -   What kinds of principles should almost every AI app include?
-   Preview the next lesson:
    -   Geniuses will create GitHub-ready READMEs and technical documentation for their capstone concepts.

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses realize that strong capstones are not built from features alone. They are built from clear purpose, useful AI behavior, and principles that guide good decisions. Day 1 sets the tone for Week 5 by helping Geniuses turn their AI system idea into a serious software product direction. By the end of the lesson, they should feel more prepared to build because they know what they are building, who it is for, and what standards should guide the work.

**Facilitator Notes**
---------------------

-   Keep using **Geniuses** consistently in examples and discussion.
-   Reinforce that principles should be practical, not vague slogans.
-   Encourage narrower, stronger product ideas over giant feature lists.
-   Help Geniuses tie their principles to real design and trust decisions.
-   Remind Geniuses that tomorrow they will turn this vision into real README and technical documentation files.
