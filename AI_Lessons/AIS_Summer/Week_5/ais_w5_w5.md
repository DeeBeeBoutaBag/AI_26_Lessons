Lesson Plan: AI Systems Week 5, Day 5 --- Pitching, Demo Story, and Capstone Launch Readiness
===========================================================================================

**Duration:**
-------------

3 hours

**Lesson Overview:**
--------------------

Day 5 is the final guided lesson before Geniuses move into their capstone build phase. After defining their product vision, writing documentation, creating trust and data policies, and planning their prototype scope, Geniuses now focus on the final step before building: **explaining the product clearly and convincingly**. This lesson centers on **pitching, demo storytelling, product communication, and capstone launch readiness**.

The lesson emphasizes that strong AI builders do not only build. They also know how to explain what they are building, why it matters, how it works, and why someone should care. Geniuses learn how to pitch an AI software product in a way that combines product thinking, technical clarity, and trust awareness. They also learn how to tell the story of a demo by showing the user, the problem, the workflow, and the value. During guided practice, the class builds a Python-based pitch and demo organizer that helps structure a product story. During independent practice, each Genius creates a `PITCH.md` and a `DEMO_STORY.md` for their capstone. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel that they are not just ready to build. They are ready to explain, defend, and present their capstone like a real AI software venture.

**Objectives:**
---------------

-   Explain the purpose of a pitch in technical product development.
-   Identify the key parts of a strong AI software pitch.
-   Distinguish between a product pitch and a demo story.
-   Build a Python-based pitch and demo organizer in VS Code.
-   Create a `PITCH.md` and a `DEMO_STORY.md` for a capstone project.
-   Practice presenting a capstone product with technical clarity, user focus, and trust awareness.
-   Reflect on how strong communication improves build confidence, product clarity, and final presentation readiness.

**Materials:**
--------------

-   Laptops for all Geniuses
-   VS Code installed
-   Python installed and working
-   Internet access
-   Projector or display
-   Whiteboard and markers
-   Slide deck for lecture and discussion
-   Reflection journal or digital notes
-   Terminal access in VS Code

-   Markdown preview in VS Code

**Standards:**
--------------

-   Computational Thinking: Organize a technical product into a clear, logical narrative.
-   Computer Science Practices: Explain an AI system's purpose, workflow, and value clearly.
-   Technical Communication: Present a software product to users, judges, mentors, or stakeholders with clarity and structure.
-   Digital Literacy: Evaluate whether an AI product pitch clearly explains usefulness, trust, and responsible design.
-   Career Readiness: Practice pitching, public speaking, structured storytelling, and product communication.
-   Technical Foundations: Connect AI system behavior to a convincing product and demo narrative.

**Lesson Activity:**
--------------------

### 1. **Introduction (Time: 25 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Energize Geniuses and introduce the idea that strong products need strong explanation.
-   Start with a fun icebreaker called **"Cool Feature or Strong Pitch?"**
-   This should be the first thing and the most fun thing to wake Geniuses up and get them ready for the lesson.

#### **Icebreaker Option A: Physical Movement**

-   Label one side of the room **"Cool Feature"** and the other side **"Strong Pitch."**
-   Read short project statements aloud and have Geniuses move to the side that best matches what the statement sounds like.
-   Example prompts:
    -   "Our app uses AI, voice, and images."
    -   "Our app helps Geniuses understand worksheets faster by combining vision and spoken support."
    -   "It can detect objects and generate things."
    -   "Our product helps coaches review player movement more clearly using visual tracking and structured feedback."
    -   "It does a lot of things with AI."
    -   "It solves one clear problem for one clear user using one strong AI workflow."
-   After each round, ask:
    -   What made this sound like a feature list instead of a pitch?
    -   What made the stronger version clearer?
    -   Did the user and problem show up?
    -   Did the value feel obvious?

#### **Icebreaker Option B: Elevator Pitch Fix-Up**

-   Put weak pitch lines on the board.
-   Ask Geniuses to improve them by adding:
    -   user
    -   problem
    -   AI behavior
    -   value
-   Discuss how small changes make a pitch much stronger.

#### **Transition**

-   Say:
    -   "A capstone becomes more real when you can explain it clearly. A strong pitch is not hype. It is clarity."
-   Ask:
    -   What should someone understand in the first 20 seconds of your pitch?
    -   What would make a demo feel meaningful instead of confusing?

### 2. **Direct Instruction (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses understand the structure of strong pitches and demo stories for AI software products.
-   Use slides to guide instruction and keep it interactive.

#### **Suggested Slide Flow**

-   **Slide 1: Why Pitching Matters**
    -   A pitch helps people understand:
        -   what the product is
        -   who it helps
        -   why it matters
        -   why AI is useful
        -   why the product is worth building
-   **Slide 2: What a Strong Pitch Includes**
    -   problem
    -   user
    -   solution
    -   AI behavior
    -   product value
    -   trust or responsibility awareness
    -   why now or why this matters
-   **Slide 3: What a Demo Story Includes**
    -   who the user is
    -   what the user needs
    -   what they do in the product
    -   what the AI does
    -   what output they get
    -   why that output is useful
-   **Slide 4: Pitch vs Demo Story**
    -   **Pitch**
        -   product story
        -   problem and value
        -   why it matters
    -   **Demo story**
        -   product walkthrough
        -   how the user interacts
        -   what the system actually does
-   **Slide 5: A Strong AI Pitch Answers**
    -   Who is the product for?
    -   What problem does it solve?
    -   Why is AI the right tool here?
    -   What does the system do?
    -   Why should someone care?
-   **Slide 6: A Strong Demo Story Answers**
    -   What does the user do first?
    -   What input do they provide?
    -   What happens next?
    -   What does the AI return?
    -   What is the result for the user?
-   **Slide 7: Common Pitch Mistakes**
    -   too many features
    -   no clear user
    -   no clear problem
    -   vague AI language
    -   no trust awareness
    -   too much jargon
    -   not enough story
-   **Slide 8: Common Demo Mistakes**
    -   clicking around without a story
    -   no clear user flow
    -   no explanation of what the AI is doing
    -   showing polish without value
    -   hiding limitations instead of naming them
-   **Slide 9: Pitching AI Responsibly**
    -   do not overclaim
    -   do not pretend the AI is perfect
    -   explain where human review still matters
    -   make the value feel real, not exaggerated
-   **Slide 10: Capstone Launch Readiness**
    -   By the end of today, Geniuses should know:
        -   what they are building
        -   what their demo proves
        -   what they will say when asked what the product does and why it matters

#### **Discussion Questions**

-   What makes a pitch strong instead of vague?
-   Why is the user story so important in a demo?
-   Why should AI products explain trust and limitations in the pitch?
-   What is the difference between showing features and showing value?
-   What makes a capstone feel presentation-ready?

#### **Reflection Prompt**

-   Have Geniuses write for 4 minutes:
    -   "If you only had 30 seconds, how would you explain what your product does and why it matters?"

### 3. **Guided Practice (Time: 50 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Help Geniuses build a Python-based pitch and demo organizer, then use it to draft `PITCH.md` and `DEMO_STORY.md`.
-   Explain that today's code-along is about organizing how the product is communicated.
-   The class will create `pitch_builder.py`.

#### **Guided Practice Build**

Create a folder called `capstone-pitch-builder`.

#### **Project Goal**

Build a Python program that:

-   asks the user for the project name
-   asks who the user is
-   asks what problem is being solved
-   asks what the AI system does
-   asks what makes the product valuable
-   asks what the demo flow should show
-   prints a structured pitch and demo summary

#### **Setup Steps**

1.  Open VS Code.
2.  Create a folder called `capstone-pitch-builder`.
3.  Create a file called `pitch_builder.py`.
4.  Create two markdown files:
    -   `PITCH.md`
    -   `DEMO_STORY.md`
5.  Run the Python file in the terminal and use the results to draft both docs.

#### **Sample Code**
```
# pitch_builder.py
# AI Systems Week 5 Day 5
# This program helps Geniuses organize a capstone pitch and demo story.

print("Welcome to the Capstone Pitch Builder\n")

project_name = input("Project name: ")
user = input("Who is the product for? ")
problem = input("What problem does it solve? ")
ai_behavior = input("What does the AI system do? ")
value = input("Why is this useful or important? ")
demo_step_1 = input("What happens first in the demo? ")
demo_step_2 = input("What happens next? ")
demo_result = input("What useful result does the user get? ")

print("\n--- Pitch Summary ---")
print(f"Project Name: {project_name}")
print(f"User: {user}")
print(f"Problem: {problem}")
print(f"AI Behavior: {ai_behavior}")
print(f"Value: {value}")

print("\n--- Demo Story Summary ---")
print(f"Step 1: {demo_step_1}")
print(f"Step 2: {demo_step_2}")
print(f"Result: {demo_result}")

print("\n--- Reflection ---")
print("What should a listener understand in the first 20 seconds?")
print("What part of the demo proves the value most clearly?")
print("What limitation should still be mentioned honestly?")
```
#### **Sample `PITCH.md`**
```
# Pitch: StudyLens AI

## One-Sentence Pitch
StudyLens AI is a multimodal academic support tool that helps Geniuses understand worksheets and diagrams through image-aware reasoning and spoken guidance.

## Problem
Students often struggle to understand visual academic materials quickly, especially when they do not have immediate support.

## User
High school Geniuses who need clearer help understanding worksheets, diagrams, and classroom materials.

## Solution
StudyLens AI allows a Genius to upload or describe a worksheet or diagram, then receive a structured explanation, helpful next step, and optional spoken support.

## Why AI
The product uses AI because it needs to interpret visual content, reason about what matters, and return support that feels more interactive and accessible than static study materials.

## Why It Matters
The product helps make academic support faster, clearer, and more accessible for Geniuses.

## Trust Note
The system is designed as a support tool, not a final academic authority. Users should still review important answers and unclear visuals.
```
#### **Sample `DEMO_STORY.md`**
```
# Demo Story: StudyLens AI

## User Story
A Genius is studying and gets stuck on a worksheet with a diagram they do not fully understand.

## Demo Flow
1. The Genius uploads or describes the worksheet or diagram.
2. The system analyzes the visual content.
3. The agent returns:
   - key findings
   - a simple explanation
   - one next step
4. The system can also speak the explanation out loud.

## What the Demo Proves
- The product solves a real academic support problem
- Vision and reasoning improve understanding
- Spoken support adds accessibility and ease of use

## Honest Limitation
The system may still misunderstand low-quality or unclear visuals, so important academic details should still be reviewed.
```
#### **Guided Practice Teaching Points**

-   Why the pitch should be short, clear, and user-centered
-   Why the demo story should follow a real user flow
-   Why AI behavior should be named in plain language
-   Why trust notes make the pitch more credible, not weaker
-   Why a strong demo proves value instead of just showing features

#### **Guided Practice Discussion**

-   What made the pitch strong?
-   What made the demo flow easy to follow?
-   Where should trust and limitations be mentioned?
-   What would make a judge remember the product?

```
# my_pitch_builder.py

# This program prints a simple capstone pitch and demo summary.

print("My Capstone Pitch Summary\n")

print(f"Demo Result: {demo_result}")
```

### 5. **Closure (Time: 20 minutes)**

-   **Objective:** Reinforce that strong AI products need strong explanation, not just strong features.
-   Bring the class together for a final share-out.
-   Ask 3--5 Geniuses to share:
    -   their project name
    -   their one-sentence pitch
    -   one key demo moment
    -   one trust or limitation note
-   Lead a closing discussion:
    -   What makes a pitch memorable?
    -   What makes a demo feel real?
    -   Why does honest communication make a capstone stronger?
-   Preview the next phase:
    -   Geniuses will move into capstone build mode with a full launch package: concept, principles, docs, trust policies, prototype plan, and pitch story.

**Reflection / Takeaways:**
---------------------------

This lesson works best when Geniuses realize that strong products do not speak for themselves automatically. Builders have to explain the problem, the user, the workflow, the value, and the limits clearly. Day 5 helps capstone ideas become more presentation-ready by turning product thinking into communication. By the end of the lesson, Geniuses should feel more ready to build because they now know how to explain what they are building and why it matters.
