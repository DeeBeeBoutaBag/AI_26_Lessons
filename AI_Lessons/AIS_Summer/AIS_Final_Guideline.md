Final Capstone Project Guidelines
=================================

**Purpose**
-----------

The Final Capstone is the culminating project for **AI Systems**. Each Genius will design, plan, build, document, and present an **AI software app and venture concept** that uses real AI system behaviors to solve a real problem for a real user or community.

This is not just a coding assignment.
This is not just a cool AI demo.
This is a **software product, technical system, and venture concept** that should feel serious, useful, and responsibly designed.

* * * * *

**Capstone Goal**
-----------------

By the end of the capstone, each Genius or team must have:

-   a clear AI software product concept
-   a defined user and problem
-   a working prototype or serious partial prototype
-   a technical system explanation
-   documentation
-   trust and data policies
-   a pitch and demo-ready presentation

* * * * *

**Required Capstone Focus**
---------------------------

Every AI Systems capstone must center on an **AI software app**.

The app should use one or more major AI Systems capabilities such as:

-   **vision**
-   **generation**
-   **agents**
-   **voice / TTS**
-   **tracking**
-   **multimodal workflows**
-   **classification or detection**
-   **reasoning over visual or text input**

Your project must explain why those AI capabilities are actually needed.

* * * * *

**Required Deliverables**
-------------------------

Each Genius or team must submit all of the following:

### **1. Product Vision**

Must clearly explain:

-   project name
-   target user
-   problem being solved
-   why the product matters
-   what kind of AI system behavior is being used
-   why AI is useful for this product

### **2. Technical Principles**

Must include at least 4 principles that guide the build.

Examples:

-   build for real user value
-   support human review
-   do not overclaim what the AI can do
-   keep outputs understandable
-   protect privacy
-   use AI only where it adds meaningful value

### **3. README**

Must include:

-   project title
-   one-sentence summary
-   user
-   problem
-   AI system behavior
-   features
-   tech stack
-   setup
-   roadmap or next steps

### **4. Technical Documentation**

Must include:

-   inputs
-   outputs
-   workflow
-   AI components used
-   dependencies
-   limitations
-   future build priorities

### **5. Data and Trust Policy**

Must include:

-   what data is used
-   what data should be limited or handled carefully
-   what the AI helps with
-   what the AI should not pretend to do
-   what users should still review
-   one major trust concern
-   one clear responsible design boundary

### **6. Prototype Plan and Build Plan**

Must include:

-   MVP definition
-   core user flow
-   core features
-   stretch features
-   what is in scope
-   what is out of scope
-   what should be built first
-   what success looks like by the end of the capstone

### **7. Working Prototype**

Must include:

-   a real software workflow
-   visible user input
-   visible AI system behavior
-   visible output
-   enough functionality to clearly prove the product idea

### **8. Final Pitch and Demo**

Must clearly explain:

-   what the product is
-   who it is for
-   what problem it solves
-   why AI is the right tool
-   how the workflow works
-   what the prototype proves
-   what trust limitations still exist
-   why the product matters

* * * * *

**Strict Capstone Guardrails**
------------------------------

These rules are not optional.

### **1. Every capstone must solve a real problem**

-   No joke products
-   No meaningless "AI for everything" apps
-   No random assistant with no clear user or purpose
-   The problem must be specific and meaningful

### **2. Every capstone must be built for a real user**

-   "Everyone" is not a valid target user
-   The user must be clearly named
-   The product must show awareness of real user needs

### **3. The capstone must be a software app**

-   It cannot only be slides
-   It cannot only be a pitch
-   It cannot only be a written concept
-   It must include a prototype or working product flow

### **4. AI must be meaningfully used**

-   AI cannot be added just to sound advanced
-   The project must explain why AI improves the product
-   If the same product works just as well without AI, that should be questioned

### **5. Every capstone must use real AI Systems thinking**

Your project should clearly use one or more of the following in a meaningful way:

-   vision
-   generation
-   agents
-   voice / TTS
-   multimodal reasoning
-   object detection
-   tracking
-   classification
-   structured AI workflow

### **6. The capstone must be appropriate and responsible**

Projects may not include:

-   harmful, dangerous, or illegal use cases
-   cheating or academic dishonesty tools
-   products designed to deceive, manipulate, or exploit
-   fake evidence, fake surveillance claims, or misleading synthetic media
-   impersonation systems
-   harmful self-harm or violence support
-   unsafe medical, legal, or financial advice systems presented as authoritative
-   products that encourage privacy invasion or unethical tracking

### **7. Trust and responsibility must be visible**

Every project must include:

-   at least one trust concern
-   at least one responsible design choice
-   at least one system limitation
-   at least one point where human review is still needed

### **8. Documentation is required**

A strong project is not just code.\
It must also be documented clearly.

No capstone is complete without:

-   README
-   technical doc
-   data/trust policy
-   prototype plan
-   pitch materials

### **9. The capstone must reflect real scope**

-   Do not design something impossibly large for the timeline
-   The MVP must be realistic
-   A strong smaller build is better than a giant unfinished one

### **10. Every Genius must contribute**

If working in a team:

-   roles must be clear
-   each Genius must contribute meaningful work
-   each Genius must be able to explain their role, system decisions, and product direction

### **11. Professionalism is required**

All work must be:

-   school-appropriate
-   respectful
-   organized
-   clearly written
-   presentation-ready
-   honest about what works and what does not yet work

* * * * *

**Explicit Rules**
------------------

Follow these exactly.

-   Your project must have a title.
-   Your project must have a one-sentence summary.
-   Your project must have a clearly defined user.
-   Your project must have a clearly defined problem.
-   Your project must explain what AI system behavior it uses.
-   Your project must explain why AI is needed.
-   Your project must include a prototype.
-   Your project must include documentation.
-   Your project must include a data and trust policy.
-   Your project must include an MVP and build plan.
-   Your project must include a final pitch and demo story.
-   Your project must show technical thinking, product thinking, and responsible AI thinking.

* * * * *

**Minimum Technical Expectations**
----------------------------------

Your capstone should use one or more of the following where appropriate:

-   Python
-   JavaScript
-   Node
-   HTML/CSS
-   OpenAI API
-   vision workflows
-   TTS workflows
-   image generation workflows
-   multimodal agent workflows

Your capstone must not rely only on:

-   slides
-   writing
-   static mockups
-   vague descriptions of future ideas

There must be a real software flow.

* * * * *

**Minimum Presentation Expectations**
-------------------------------------

Your final presentation must answer:

1.  What is your product?
2.  Who is it for?
3.  What problem does it solve?
4.  Why is this problem important?
5.  What AI system behavior does it use?
6.  Why is AI the right tool here?
7.  How does the workflow work?
8.  What does the prototype currently do?
9.  What should users still review or be careful about?
10. What is the long-term vision for the product?

* * * * *

**What Strong AI Systems Capstones Usually Show**
-------------------------------------------------

-   a clear user
-   a clear problem
-   a real software workflow
-   meaningful AI use
-   strong system explanation
-   focused MVP scope
-   real product value
-   documentation
-   trust and policy thinking
-   honest limitations
-   a strong pitch and demo story

* * * * *

**Automatic Weaknesses That Will Hurt the Project**
---------------------------------------------------

These will lower the strength of the capstone:

-   vague user
-   vague problem
-   too many features with no focus
-   AI added with no reason
-   no prototype
-   no README
-   no trust policy
-   no clear system explanation
-   misleading claims about what the AI can do
-   no honest limitations
-   no clear MVP
-   no product value beyond "it looks cool"

* * * * *

**Capstone Standard**
---------------------

A Final Capstone should feel like:

-   a real AI software product in progress
-   a system someone could understand and evaluate
-   a prototype with clear value
-   a venture that could be pitched seriously
-   a product built with trust and responsibility in mind

Not just:

-   a random AI experiment
-   a flashy demo with no clear use case
-   a big idea with no working flow
-   a product that overpromises what AI can do

* * * * *

**Final Reminder**
------------------

The goal is not perfection.
The goal is to build something **real, useful, technically thoughtful, and responsibly designed**.

A strong AI Systems capstone should show that a Genius can:

-   understand modern AI systems
-   turn them into software
-   explain them clearly
-   build with trust
-   present them seriously
